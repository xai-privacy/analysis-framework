# Question 2023_21

## Original Question

Determinism is the view that all events, including human mental states and actions, are completely determined by prior events. Under determinism, is it possible to ascribe moral responsibility to an agent for what they have done? The view that this is not possible is called incompatibilism, which argues that accepting determinism leaves no room for free will. On the other hand, the view that it is still possible to ascribe moral responsibility to someone even if determinism is accepted is called compatibilism. Compatibilists believe that if the agent's mental state is the cause of the action, it can be said that the agent has influenced the occurrence of the action, and that is sufficient for imposing moral responsibility. Alice, a compatibilist, judged that people advocate incompatibilism because they fail to grasp exactly this point. Therefore, Alice proposed the following hypothesis.<Hypothesis>If, even in a deterministic world, one acknowledges that the agent's mental state can influence the occurrence of an action, the likelihood of accepting compatibilism increases.To test this hypothesis, Alice asked 100 experimental subjects to respond to the following two statements, with only one answer each—either agree or disagree—assuming that the universe described in the scenario actually exists.<Scenario>There is a universe in which the entire process of creation and extinction is endlessly repeated. In this universe, it is determined that every event occurs in exactly the same way each time the process is repeated. In this universe, a person named Tom decides at a particular time to steal a particular ring and actually does so. Each time the process is repeated, Tom makes the same decision and acts in the same way.[Statement 1] Tom’s decision to steal the ring influences his action of stealing the ring.[Statement 2] Tom is morally responsible for stealing the ring. <question> Which of the following evaluations of the above text is/are correct as listed in <statements>? <statements> (a) Anyone who disagrees with [Statement 1] is an incompatibilist, and anyone who agrees with [Statement 2] is a compatibilist.(b) If there are far more experimental subjects who agree with both [Statement 1] and [Statement 2] than those who agree with neither statement, the <Hypothesis> is strengthened.(c) If out of the 50 subjects who did not agree with [Statement 2], almost all agreed with [Statement 1], and out of the 50 who agreed with [Statement 2], almost all did not agree with [Statement 1], then the <Hypothesis> is weakened. <choices>① (a) ② (c) ③ (a), (b) ④ (b), (c) ⑤ (a), (b), (c)

## Original Answer

**Answer choice:** 2

Since only (c) is a correct evaluation among the given options, the answer is ②.

## LLM Response

Answer-④

Rationale 1. Statement (a) is correct because if someone disagrees with Statement 1, it implies that they believe the decision does not influence the action, which is a key point of incompatibilism. On the other hand, if someone agrees with Statement 2, it implies that they believe the decision does influence the action, which is a key point of compatibilism.

Rationale 2. Statement (b) is correct because if there are significantly more experimental subjects who agree with both statements, it strengthens the hypothesis that the more one acknowledges the agent's mental state can influence the action, the more likely they are to accept compatibilism.

Rationale 3. Statement (c) is correct because if out of the 50 subjects who did not agree with Statement 2, almost all agreed with Statement 1, and out of the 50 who agreed with Statement 2, almost all did not agree with Statement 1, then it weakens the hypothesis because this suggests that the subjects who disagree with Statement 2 are not willing to accept the influence of the agent's mental state on the action.
