# Question 2021_03

## Original Question

Country X and Country Y operate a system where bankruptcy can be declared by the court upon the application of either the creditor or the debtor. Country X: If an individual cannot repay borrowed money, they may declare bankruptcy, and all assets they possess at the time of bankruptcy are distributed to the creditors. Any remaining debts are fully discharged. The court grants debt discharge except in cases where the debtor maliciously fails to pay despite having the ability to do so. A person who has been discharged through bankruptcy faces no restrictions on the freedoms and rights they enjoy as a citizen in daily life.Country Y: If an individual cannot repay borrowed money, they may declare bankruptcy, and all assets possessed at the time of bankruptcy are distributed to the creditors. However, unless the creditor agrees, debts to that creditor are not discharged. Creditors may exercise their rights without restriction over assets acquired by the debtor after bankruptcy until their full debt is repaid. The bankrupt individual is detained for a certain period, loses the right to vote until the debts are paid in full, and the bankruptcy record is made public. <question> Which of the following is NOT a correct analysis of the above passage? <choices> ① Someone who is concerned that creditors will continue to urge bankrupt debtors to repay their debts and that related lawsuits will never cease would support Country X's system. ② Someone who believes that individuals are rational beings who make and are responsible for their own decisions, and that the state has no need to help those who have recklessly overspent, would oppose Country X's system. ③ Someone who worries that creditors may use bankruptcy filings as a means of threat in order to collect their claims ahead of other creditors would support Country Y's system. ④ Someone who fears that individuals at risk of bankruptcy will keep taking on more debt to repay existing debt, thereby perpetuating creditor–debtor relationships, would oppose Country Y's system. ⑤ Someone who is concerned that making creditor–debtor relationships after bankruptcy subject to the will of the creditor would cause social confusion, such as the emergence of fictitious creditors, would oppose Country Y's system.

## Original Answer

**Answer choice:** 3

Only ③ is the incorrect analysis among the <choices>, so the answer is ③.

## LLM Response

Answer-⑤. Rationale 5. The passage does not mention anything regarding the social impact of the system in terms of fictitious creditors.
