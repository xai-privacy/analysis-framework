## Original Question

Some scholars argue that the Korean connective "또는" ("or") is an ambiguous term used to express two different types of meaning. According to this position, the literal meaning of "또는" differs in the following two sentences: (1) Cheolsu has a laptop or a cell phone. (2) As dessert, we offer coffee or green tea. In (1), "또는" is used in an inclusive sense, which is true even if Cheolsu has both a laptop and a cell phone. In contrast, in (2), "또는" is used in an exclusive sense, so the sentence would be false if both coffee and green tea are served as dessert. However, this argument fails to distinguish between "literal meaning and implicature," and if we make this distinction, we can develop a theory that "또는" is not, in fact, an ambiguous term. Consider the following sentence: (3) Some members can attend the party. The literal meaning expressed by sentence (3) is that <some members can attend the party>. However, in many instances where (3) is used, the speaker's intention in using the word "some" is to also convey to the listener that <not all members can attend the party>. Yet, this is conveyed as implicature, not as literal meaning. The reason is that, unlike the literal meaning, in the case of implicature transmitted in specific contexts, even if we list a sentence expressing the negation of that implied content after the original sentence, no logical contradiction arises between the two sentences. In other words, "Some members can attend the party. Of course, it is also possible that all members can attend the party." does not produce any contradiction. In a similar vein, it is possible to claim that "the literal meaning of '또는' is only inclusive, and the exclusive meaning is conveyed as implicature." For example, "As dessert, we offer coffee or green tea. Of course, we could also offer both." does not result in any contradiction, and therefore, we can conclude that the content <You cannot have both coffee and green tea as dessert> conveyed in the use of (2) is not the literal meaning of the original sentence, but is rather an implicature. <question> Which of the following analyses about the above passage is/are correct according to <statements>? <statements> (a) The statement, "When p, q, r, and s are all true sentences, if the sentence 'p or q' is true but the sentence 'r or s' is false, then the 'or' used in the former and latter sentences represents different meanings," does not conflict with this position. (b) According to the author’s explanation of literal meaning and implicature, if we use the sentence "Cheolsu ate rice and bread," we cannot convey the content <Cheolsu ate bread> as implicature. (c) According to the diagnosis that "the literal meaning of 'or' is only the inclusive meaning, and the exclusive meaning is conveyed as implicature," the content <it is possible to have both coffee and green tea as dessert> is not included in the literal meaning of (2) but is conveyed as implicature. <choices> ① (a) ② (c) ③ (a), (b) ④ (b), (c) ⑤ (a), (b), (c)
