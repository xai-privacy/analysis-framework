# Question 2022_05

## Original Question

Eighty years ago, Island K was unlawfully occupied by the state, resulting in the forced expulsion of the indigenous resident, Alice, from Island K, after which she struggled to live in a foreign land. If Alice were still alive, she should be compensated for the wrong committed by the state; however, Alice has already passed away. A debate arose as to whether compensation should now be made to Alice's surviving child, Bob. The following arguments were presented:Alice: Compensation should be given to the person affected by the wrongful act. ⓐ To compensate person S for wrongful act X is to provide S with benefits such that S achieves the standard of living that they would have enjoyed had X not occurred. Compensation should only occur when the victim’s standard of living has been worsened, and only in such cases. Therefore, compensation should be given to Bob to the extent that his standard of living would have been, had the unlawful occupation of Island K eighty years ago not occurred.Bob: There is a serious problem with Alice’s argument. Had the unlawful occupation of Island K not occurred, Bob’s father would not have met Bob’s mother, but instead would have met another woman, resulting in a completely different child being born—and Bob would not have existed at all. Therefore, it is incorrect to say that Bob would have enjoyed a higher standard of living if the unlawful occupation hadn’t happened; in fact, there is simply no answer to the question of what Bob’s standard of living would have been in that alternate scenario.Charlie: The wrongful act that gives rise to Bob's claim to compensation is not the unlawful occupation of Island K eighty years ago, but rather the fact that, at some point after Bob was born, no compensation was given to Alice for the occupation of Island K. If this had not been the case—in other words, if compensation had been given to Alice for the occupation after Bob was born—then Alice would have been able to provide Bob with better educational opportunities and resources, and Bob would have had a better life. Since such compensation was not given in the past, Bob must be compensated now. <question> Which of the following analyses of this debate are correct according to the <statements> below? <statements> (a) If Alice comes to accept that “Although Alice herself would have enjoyed a far higher standard of living than she actually did had the unlawful occupation of Island K not occurred eighty years ago, Bob would, on the contrary, have enjoyed a lower standard of living,” then she would not agree that compensation should be given to Bob.(b) If Bob accepts principle ⓐ, then he would agree that compensation should be made to Bob for the unlawful occupation of Island K eighty years ago.(c) Charlie does not agree with principle ⓐ, but agrees with Alice in that compensation should be made to Bob. <choices>① (a) ② (b) ③ (a), (c) ④ (b), (c) ⑤ (a), (b), (c)

## Original Answer

**Answer choice:** 1

Since (a) alone is the correct analysis, the answer is ①.

## LLM Response

Answer-④

Rationale 1. The analysis provided in statement ⓐ is correct. If Alice accepts that Bob would have enjoyed a lower standard of living than he actually did if the unlawful occupation of Island K had not occurred, it logically follows that she would agree that compensation should not be made to Bob.

Rationale 2. The analysis provided in statement ⓐ is correct. If Bob accepts principle ⓐ, which states that compensation should only be made to the person whose standard of living would have been improved if the wrongful act had not occurred, it logically follows that Bob would agree that compensation should not be made to him.

Rationale 3. The analysis provided in statement ⓐ is correct. Charlie agrees with Alice that compensation should be made to Bob, but he does not agree with principle ⓐ.
