# Question 2023_09

## Original Question

[Regulations] Article 1 (Definition): The term “pharmacist” refers to matters related to the manufacturing, compounding, storage, import, sale (including delivery), and other pharmaceutical techniques of drugs and quasi-drugs. Article 2 (Sale of Drugs): Unless you are a pharmacy founder, you cannot sell or acquire drugs for the purpose of sale. However, this does not apply if a person who has obtained a manufacturing license sells the drugs they manufactured to a person who has obtained a license for manufacturing or selling drugs. <Case> Company P has obtained licenses for both drug manufacturing and drug sales and thus simultaneously holds the status of both a drug manufacturer and a drug wholesaler. Company P was subject to a suspension of drug sales in its capacity as a manufacturer due to a violation of drug handling methods. With regard to this, there are competing views as to whether Company P shipping drugs from its manufacturer status and receiving them as a wholesaler constitutes a “sale” under these regulations.<Opinions>View 1: Article 2 is intended to strictly manage and prevent the abnormal distribution of drugs. Even if a pharmaceutical company obtains both a manufacturing license and a wholesaler license, if drugs are moved from the manufacturer side to the wholesaler side, it can be regarded as a transaction between entities with separate statuses, thus constituting a “sale."View 2: Generally, “sale" means transferring goods to another person in exchange for a price, presupposing a giver and a recipient. Even if a pharmaceutical company has both manufacturing and wholesaler licenses, when receiving and managing drugs manufactured under one’s manufacturing status within the same company as a wholesaler, it is merely an internal transfer and not a transaction with an independent third party; thus, it does not constitute a “sale." <question> Which of the following in <statements> is/are correct as assessments of the <opinions>? <statements> (a) If there are separate regulations stating that a person intending to be a drug wholesaler must obtain permission from the mayor, county governor, or district head, while a person intending to be a manufacturer must obtain permission from the Food and Drug Administration’s head, then View 1 is weakened.(b) If the concept of “delivery” included in “sale” under Article 1 encompasses the transfer of a thing itself irrespective of the transaction counterparty, then View 2 is strengthened.(c) If, according to the purpose of Article 2, the concept of sale is to mean distribution of drugs to the general public, then View 2 is strengthened. <choices>① (b) ② (c) ③ (a), (b) ④ (a), (c) ⑤ (a), (b), (c)

## Original Answer

**Answer choice:** 2

Of the given statements, only (c) is correct, so the answer is ②.

## LLM Response

Answer-③

Rationale 1. The statements are trying to analyze the perspectives presented by the two views on the meaning of "sale" under the drug regulations. Statement (a) suggests that if there are separate requirements for obtaining licenses as a manufacturer and wholesaler, then View 1 is weakened, implying that the distinction between these roles might affect the interpretation of "sale" as a transaction between separate entities. This is a valid point since the specific regulations could provide clarity on the distinction between these roles and the applicability of View 1.

Rationale 2. Statement (b) states that if the concept of "delivery" included in "sale" under Article 1 encompasses the transfer of a thing itself irrespective of the transaction counterparty, then View 2 is strengthened. This is true because if the definition of "delivery" includes the transfer of goods regardless of the transaction partner, it would support the interpretation that receiving drugs manufactured under one's manufacturing status within the same company as a wholesaler is merely an internal transfer and not a transaction with an independent third party, thereby supporting View 2.

Rationale 3. Statement (c) suggests that if the purpose of Article 2 is to distribute drugs to the general public, then View 2 is strengthened. This is also a valid point since if the primary purpose of Article 2 is to regulate the distribution of drugs to the general public, then receiving drugs within the same company as a wholesaler would not be considered a sale, as it is not intended for external distribution. This supports the interpretation presented in View 2.
