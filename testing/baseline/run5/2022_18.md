## Original Question

Alice: My child, the exam is tomorrow. Why aren't you studying? Bob: Mother, that’s a good question. I have chosen not to study. Alice: Could you give a convincing explanation as to why you’ve made such a surprising choice? Bob: The exam I am taking is designed to rank all test-takers from first to last place. I found out that other test-takers are happy even if their rank goes up just a little. But I do not feel any joy when my rank goes up. And I want more people to experience happiness. So, wouldn't my not studying make other test-takers happy? If I don't study, more students will move up in the rankings. Therefore, not studying is justified. Alice: You’re saying that just by not studying, you can somehow cause others to be happy? It seems to me you’re making a remarkable claim that you can do nothing and yet make others happy. If others are happy because of their own rank, isn't that because they studied? It has nothing to do with you not doing anything. Bob: That's not true, Mother. If I were to study, more people would get a score lower than mine, compared to if I did not study. (c) For Alice’s claim that not studying cannot be the cause of others’ happiness to be true, it must be assumed that not doing something cannot be the cause of anything else. <choices> ① (a) ② (b) ③ (a), (c) ④ (b), (c) ⑤ (a), (b), (c)

## Original Answer

**Answer choice:** 1

Therefore, only (a) is the correct analysis, and the answer is ①.

## LLM Response

Answer-③ (a), (c)

Rationale 1. (a) If not every means of attaining what one wants is justified just because one desires it, then Bob's argument is weakened. This statement is correct because it emphasizes the importance of evaluating the moral implications of one's actions, rather than simply following one's desires. Bob's argument relies on the idea that not studying will cause others to be happy, but this assumption neglects the fact that others' happiness is often the result of their own actions, such as studying hard.

Rationale 2. (c) For Alice's claim that not studying cannot be the cause of others' happiness to be true, it must be assumed that not doing something cannot be the cause of anything else. This statement is correct because it highlights the importance of distinguishing between causes and consequences. If not studying does not directly cause others' happiness, then it cannot be justified solely based on the potential happiness it may cause others. This distinction is essential in evaluating the morality of Bob's decision.
