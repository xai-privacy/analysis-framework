# Question 2023_35

## Original Question

A program X has been developed that can engage in everyday Korean conversation. Alice, Bob, and Charlie are debating whether there is a fundamental difference between X's Korean language ability and that of a native Korean speaker. Alice: Let’s suppose there is an Englishman named Roy who can remember everything he hears. To Roy, who knows no Korean at all, we play all the possible everyday Korean conversations that X can produce. Now, Roy has memorized virtually every possible combination of sequentially related Korean words and sentences that might occur in daily conversation. Any ordinary Korean who converses with Roy would think that Roy’s Korean is fluent. However, Roy does not understand the meaning of Korean. Since X’s Korean ability is similar to Roy’s, there is a fundamental difference between X and native speakers of Korean in terms of Korean language ability. Bob: Let’s assume that a neuroscientist named Minsoo has implanted a neural program into his brain. This neural program is designed to find solutions to various equations using mathematical symbols, such as numbers and operators, and it works so that as soon as Minsoo looks at a formula, the solution immediately appears in his consciousness. There is essentially no difference between Minsoo obtaining answers to equations directly and the neural program doing so. However, unlike Minsoo, the neural program itself does not understand the meaning of mathematical symbols—for example, the meaning of the numeral 0. The way X uses Korean is, in principle, no different from the way the neural program solves mathematical equations. Therefore, X’s Korean language ability and the neural program’s mathematical ability are similar. Thus, X and native Korean speakers are fundamentally the same in terms of Korean language ability. Charlie: Of course, X itself does not understand the meaning of Korean. But let’s consider Robot R, which is equipped with X, and can perceive the world and act accordingly through various sensory sensors. In this case, for example, if R actually sees a real sweet potato, the symbol 'sweet potato' would be activated in R’s electronic brain. Not only can R engage in everyday Korean conversation, it can also appropriately respond to real objects or phenomena indicated by Korean words or sentences. Understanding the meaning of Korean doesn’t seem to be any different from this. <question> Which of the following is correctly inferred from the above? Choose all that apply from <statements>. <statements> (a) According to Alice, if Roy and R actually see a potato, both would recall the symbol ‘potato.’ (b) Bob would agree that there is no fundamental difference in Korean language ability between R and a native Korean speaker. (c) Alice and Bob would agree that X does not understand the meaning of Korean. <choices> ① (a) ② (c) ③ (a), (b) ④ (b), (c) ⑤ (a), (b), (c)

## Original Answer

**Answer choice:** 4

Thus, only (b) and (c) are correct inferences, making the answer ④.

## LLM Response

Answer-⑤ (a), (b), (c)

Rationale 1. Statement (a) is correct because, according to Alice, if Roy and R actually see a potato, both would recall the symbol ‘potato.’ This is because Roy has memorized virtually every possible combination of sequentially related Korean words and sentences that might occur in daily conversation, including the sentence associated with the word "potato."

Rationale 2. Statement (b) is correct because, according to Bob, there is essentially no difference between R and a native Korean speaker in terms of Korean language ability. Both R and a native Korean speaker can engage in everyday Korean conversation and understand the meaning of Korean.

Rationale 3. Statement (c) is correct because, according to both Alice and Bob, X does not understand the meaning of Korean. This is because X's Korean ability is similar to Roy's, and X's ability to understand the meaning of Korean is not similar to a native Korean speaker's ability.
