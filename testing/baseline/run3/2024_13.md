# Question 2024_13

## Original Question

According to our intuitions, murder cannot be morally justified, and the murderer is morally responsible. Let us consider the following two situations. (Situation 1) Alice, the head of a mafia organization planning a bank robbery, instructs Bob, a member of the organization, to kill the bank's security officer. Bob, following Alice's instructions, shoots and kills the security officer. (Situation 2) During a fierce battle with the enemy, commander Charlie orders his subordinate Dave to shoot and kill an enemy sniper. Dave, following Charlie's orders, kills the enemy sniper. Concerning the principles for assessing moral responsibility for Bob's and Dave's actions in the two situations above, there are the following two viewpoints. <Viewpoints> A: There is no difference between (Situation 1) and (Situation 2) in that an act of killing has occurred. Therefore, there is no reason that the principle for evaluating moral responsibility for murder should be different between (Situation 1) and (Situation 2). Let us define principle P for evaluating moral responsibility as follows: "Any killing, command to kill, or execution of a killing that is not self-defense is morally impermissible, and there is moral responsibility for such an act." When evaluating the moral responsibility for the killings committed by Bob in (Situation 1) and Dave in (Situation 2), principle P should be equally applicable. B: Violence in war and violence in everyday life are clearly different; therefore, a special moral principle, distinct from the principle used to assess moral responsibility for murder in daily life, is required in war. Thus, in order to assess the moral responsibility for the actions of Bob in (Situation 1) and Dave in (Situation 2), at least two principles are necessary. To properly assess moral responsibility for killing in war, one should be able to apply a different moral principle than the one used in daily life. <question> Which of the following, among the analysis of the <Viewpoints>, are correct as they are stated in the <statements>? <statements> (a) If, according to P, Bob is morally responsible but Dave is not, viewpoint A is weakened. (b) According to A, in order to argue that Dave bears moral responsibility for the killing in (Situation 2) under P, it must be proven that Dave’s action does not count as self-defense. (c) According to B, the conclusion that both Bob and Dave are morally responsible cannot be reached. <choices> ① (b) ② (c) ③ (a), (b) ④ (a), (c) ⑤ (a), (b), (c)

## Original Answer

**Answer choice:** 1

Only (b) is the correct analysis, so the answer is ①.

## LLM Response

Answer-⑤

1. (b) is correct. If, according to P, Bob is morally responsible but Dave is not, it would indicate that P only holds for situations in which there is no self-defense. This would weaken viewpoint A, which says that principle P should be equally applicable to both situations. If the situation in (Situation 1) does not count as a case of self-defense, then principle P would not hold for that situation, which indicates that there is no difference between the situations, and therefore, viewpoint A is weakened.
2. (c) is correct. Viewpoint B says that a special moral principle is required in war, so if one of the viewpoints has a reason that it is impossible to determine whether the actions of Bob or Dave are morally permissible under that special principle, then it would be impossible to conclude that both Bob and Dave are morally responsible, and the conclusion that both are morally responsible cannot be reached.
