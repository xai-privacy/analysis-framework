# Question 2023_23

## Original Question

According to Theory A on pleasure, pleasure is a kind of sensation that we like—in other words, pleasure itself—while pain is an unpleasant sensation that we dislike. On the other hand, according to Theory B, pleasure has nothing to do with the sensation we feel; rather, it arises when a subject’s abilities and relevant conditions are well-matched with the object in which those abilities are exercised. In summary, to act with pleasure means that the subject is performing work that matches their ability under good conditions and doing so excellently. In contrast, pain arises when the subject’s ability and conditions are not well-matched with the object of their ability. Theory A appeals to the feeling that accompanies pleasure and pain, which gives it intuitive persuasive power. However, Theory B argues that pleasure and pain should not be explained as sensations, but rather by the fitness that characterizes pleasurable or painful activity. In a recent interview, three exceptional mathematicians—Alice, Bob, and Charlie—who solved a long-standing mathematical problem, spoke as follows about the pleasure of mathematics. Alice: I had been preparing for a long time to solve this age-old problem. I worked on it systematically and focused all my efforts. Of course, there were many challenges and moments of frustration. At times, it was painful. But believing in myself and the process of solving the problem was truly a pleasurable experience. Bob: After years of intensive effort, I finally solved this problem. That moment was exhilarating—because I had worked so hard for it. However, the pain I felt during the long period of confusion outweighed that moment of pleasure. In fact, I feel more pleasure when I solve many easy problems that don’t require concentration. Charlie: Do I find mathematics pleasurable? Just as there are no students who study simply because they love studying, for me, mathematics is just a job. Especially when I wrestled with difficult problems, it was painful. I worked hard out of a sense of duty, which made me good at mathematics, and in the end, I was able to solve that problem through focused effort. <question> Which of the following is the most appropriate analysis of the above passage? <statements> (1) According to Theory A, neither Bob nor Charlie experienced pleasure when solving difficult problems through intense focus. (2) According to Theory B, the pleasure Bob feels when solving easy problems is no less than the pleasure Alice experienced. (3) According to both Theory A and B, the pain Bob recounts is considered pleasure. (4) According to both Theory A and B, the experience Bob had solving easy problems was pleasurable. (5) According to Theory A, mathematics is not pleasurable for Charlie; however, according to Theory B, mathematics is a pleasurable activity for Charlie. <choices> ①(1) ②(2) ③(3) ④(4) ⑤(5).

## Original Answer

**Answer choice:** 5

Only ⑤ is the correct analysis among the <choices>, so the answer is ⑤.

## LLM Response

This contradicts the idea that pain is an unpleasant sensation that we dislike. Therefore, option ① is incorrect. 2. According to Theory B, pleasure arises from the fitness that characterizes pleasurable activity. Charlie describes mathematics as "just a job" and only works hard out of a sense of duty, which doesn't fit the theory's description of a pleasurable activity. Therefore, option ② is incorrect.
