# Question 2022_16

## Original Question

Socrates: Regarding an object, isn’t it that we either know it or do not know it, one of the two? Then, necessarily, a person making a judgment must be judging about something they know or about something they do not know, correct?Theaetetus: That is necessary.Socrates: And it is impossible for someone to both know and not know an object at the same time, or to not know and know at the same time.Theaetetus: That is so.Socrates: Then, if someone who makes a false judgment is someone who knows the object of their judgment, does that mean that he considers something he knows not as itself, but as something else that he knows? So, even though he knows both, does he end up not knowing both again?Theaetetus: That is impossible.Socrates: If someone who makes a false judgment does not know the object of their judgment, does that mean he thinks of what he does not know as some other thing he does not know? So, is it possible for someone who does not know either you or me to come to the idea that “Socrates is Theaetetus” or “Theaetetus is Socrates”?Theaetetus: How could that be possible?Socrates: Indeed, there is never a case where someone considers what they know to be something they do not know, nor is there a case where someone considers what they do not know to be something they know. So then, how could one make a false judgment? Because we either know an object or we do not know it, and in either case, making a false judgment seems impossible. <question> Which of the following analyses of the dialogue above are correct, as stated in the <statements>? <statements> (a) According to Socrates, even if one knows only 'a' and does not know 'b', one can still make a true judgment such as “a is b.”(b) According to Socrates, if one knows neither 'a' nor 'b', one cannot even make a false judgment such as “a is b.”(c) If it is actually possible to make a false judgment such as “a is b” while knowing both 'a' and 'b', then Socrates’ argument loses its persuasiveness. <choices> ① (a) ② (c) ③ (a), (b) ④ (b), (c) ⑤ (a), (b), (c)

## Original Answer

**Answer choice:** 4

Since only (b) and (c) are correct, the answer is ④.

## LLM Response

Answer-④

Rationale 1. (a) is incorrect because Socrates argued that even if one knows only 'a' and does not know 'b', one cannot make a true judgment such as “a is b.” (explanation) According to Socrates, it is impossible to make a true judgment if one does not know both 'a' and 'b'. (explanation) If one only knows 'a', it means one does not know 'b' at all, so the judgment cannot be true. (explanation)

Rationale 2. (b) is correct because Socrates said that if one knows neither 'a' nor 'b', one cannot even make a false judgment such as “a is b.” (explanation) Socrates concluded that a person who makes a false judgment must be judging about something they know or about something they do not know. (explanation) If one knows neither 'a' nor 'b', it is impossible to make a false judgment. (explanation)

Rationale 3. (c) is incorrect because Socrates’ argument does not lose its persuasiveness if it is possible to make a false judgment. (explanation) Socrates’ conclusion that it is impossible to make a false judgment is based on the fact that a person must know or not know an object to make a judgment. (explanation) If making a false judgment is impossible, it means that Socrates’ argument is correct. (explanation)
