## Original question

A contract is established by the autonomous agreement of the parties. In light of the nature and function of contracts, contract law must not only recognize the autonomy of the parties but also be a normative system that maximally implements and strengthens such autonomy. In what cases can the autonomy of the parties be restricted? There are three views regarding this question. A: Autonomy should be protected because the value lies in autonomy itself rather than the consequences it brings. Since the intention of the parties is inherently worthy of respect, even if the intention displayed by one party differs from what the party originally intended and the other party relies on the displayed intention, the originally intended will should be respected over what was expressed. State intervention is justified only when the exercise of autonomy so fundamentally infringes upon autonomy itself. B: Autonomy should be protected by focusing on the utility it brings to the parties, rather than the value of autonomy itself.
