# Question 2025_17

## Original Question

Let us ask a participant in a bet to choose between $1 and $10, instructing them to make a choice that they will later regret. The participant is informed in advance that only if they make a regretful choice will they be awarded an additional $100. Choosing the option that yields more money is considered to be the rational choice, for which there will be no regret; any other choice is considered a regretful one. At first glance, "ⓐ the rational choice in this bet" appears to be selecting $1. This is because giving up $10 to take $1 would be a regretful choice, and thus the $100 would be awarded. However, if this is the case, choosing $1 would no longer be a regretful choice, so the $100 would not actually be paid out. Although the choice is made only once, whether it is regarded as a regretful choice changes continuously, causing the payment or non-payment of the $100 to endlessly oscillate between the two. The same thing happens if $10 is chosen. Since choosing $10 instead of $1 is not a regretful choice, the $100 is not paid. In that case, however, this choice becomes a regretful one, so the $100 should then be paid; but then, the choice of $10 again becomes not a regretful choice. Here too, the $100 endlessly oscillates between being awarded and not awarded. Thus, it is impossible to say what the rational choice is in this bet. Let us examine the structure of this bet, following the flow from the point at which one contemplates what choice to make (point 0) through to the point at which one looks back on one’s choice (point 2). The moment the choice between $1 and $10 is made, point 1, lies between points 0 and 2. The intention behind this choice is to experience regret at point 2. The object of this intention is a future event that has not yet occurred. In contrast, regret concerns the past, which has already happened. The participant in this bet intends at point 0 to regret at point 2 the choice made at point 1. But precisely because this intention is realized, the regretful choice made at point 1 becomes a non-regretful choice. And this process repeats endlessly. "ⓑ The intention when viewing from point 0 to point 2" and "the regret when looking back from point 2 to point 1" lead to a paradoxical outcome. <question> Which of the following analyses about the above passage is correct? Choose every correct statement from <statements>. <statements> (a) If the rules of the bet are changed so that the additional $100 is awarded no matter which choice between $1 and $10 the participant makes, then making a regretful choice becomes impossible. (b) If the rules of the bet are changed so that the additional $100 is awarded only if one does not regret their choice, then ⓐ is to choose $10. (c) For both choosing $1 and choosing $10 in this bet, the intention at ⓑ is the same: to bring about regret at (the subsequent point). <choices> ① (a) ② (b) ③ (a), (c) ④ (b), (c) ⑤ (a), (b), (c)

## Original Answer

**Answer choice:** 4

Since only (b) and (c) are correct in <choices>, the answer is ④.

## LLM Response

Answer-④

Rationale 1. (a) is correct because if the additional $100 is awarded regardless of the choice, then making a regretful choice becomes impossible. If the additional $100 is awarded for making a regretful choice, then the choice is not regretful, so it cannot be regretful, and therefore it cannot be awarded the $100.

Rationale 2. (b) is incorrect because if the additional $100 is awarded only if one does not regret their choice, then it is not possible to make a regretful choice that would be awarded the $100. The choice is always non-regretful, and therefore it is not possible to regret it.

Rationale 3. (c) is correct because for both choosing $1 and choosing $10 in this bet, the intention at point ⓑ is the same: to bring about regret at point 2. The participant intends to regret the choice made at point 1, regardless of whether it is $1 or $10.
