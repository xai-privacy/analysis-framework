## Original Rationale

**Preliminaries**

The passages excerpt the core of the classic debate between Clifford and James. Clifford argues that it is wrong to form beliefs based on insufficient evidence. His argument is that, if one comes to form beliefs on insufficient evidence, society becomes more susceptible to being deceived, regressing to a barbaric state as a result.James emphasizes that the pursuit of truth and the avoidance of error are distinct intellectual tasks, pointing out that Clifford’s stance is justifiable only if one prioritizes the avoidance of error—and that the choice between the two is ultimately a matter of sentiment, not evidence.

**Statement (a) Rationale**

(a) Alice’s conclusion is "To believe something on insufficient evidence is always, everywhere, and for anyone, wrong." She justifies this by arguing that not following this leads society into a 'state of barbarism.' Thus, (a) is a correct analysis.

**Statement (b) Rationale**

(b) Bob argues that there are two epistemic duties, "Believe the truth!" and "Avoid error!", and that Clifford focuses only on the latter. He also claims that this choice is grounded not in evidence but in sentiment. Therefore, according to Bob, Clifford’s belief in ⓐ is not based on sufficient evidence. Thus, (b) is a correct analysis.

**Statement (c) Rationale**

(c) Bob’s argument is unrelated to the claim that "even beliefs based on sufficient evidence can be mistaken." Even if that statement is false, that is, even if beliefs based on sufficient evidence could never be mistaken, Bob’s argument remains unaffected. He merely points out that the disposition to base belief only on sufficient evidence is itself grounded in sentiment. Therefore, (c) is not a correct analysis.

**Answer**

Since only (a) and (b) are correct, the answer is ③.
