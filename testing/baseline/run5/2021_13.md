# Question 2021_13

## Original Question

Alice is planning to speed on the highway today. There are no environmental factors that could frustrate this plan, and Alice’s determination is firm. In addition, once Alice makes up her mind, she never changes it. 'ⓐ Knowing all this, the police know that Alice will speed on the highway today.' In fact, Alice did speed on the highway. In this case, is it justified for the police to impose a speeding fine on Alice before she enters the highway? In other words, is it justified to punish someone in advance simply because it is known that they will commit an act that has not yet occurred? A: The significance of punishment is to inflict harm on a person equivalent to the wrongdoing they have committed, thus maintaining a balance. As long as it is known that a wrongdoing will be committed, the exact timing of the punishment is not important. It is the same principle as whether you pay for a TV before or after you receive it — it makes no difference. Regardless of whether the police impose the fine in advance, Alice will undoubtedly speed, and the police know this. Therefore, the police’s pre-emptive punishment to maintain balance in response to this can be justified.B: Punishing an innocent person cannot be justified under any circumstance. Even if Alice’s determination is strong, it cannot be denied that she has the ability to change her mind at the last moment and not speed. As long as Alice has that ability, before she actually speeds, she is clearly an innocent person. ## LLM Response

Answer-⑤

1. The conclusion of A does not follow if ⓐ is false because the argument of A relies on the premise that it is known that Alice will speed, and if this premise is false, then it cannot be concluded that pre-emptive punishment is justified. 
2. The view that it is impossible for others to know in advance whether an agent will perform an act if the agent has the ability to decide whether or not to perform the act is incompatible with the argument of B, which assumes that Alice will undoubtedly speed and therefore pre-emptive punishment is justified. 
3. The case of the terrorist is not similar to the case of Alice because it does not involve a situation in which the agent has the ability to decide not to perform the act. If the terrorist does not have this ability, then it is not clear whether pre-emptive punishment would be justified, and the argument of A is not applicable in this case.
