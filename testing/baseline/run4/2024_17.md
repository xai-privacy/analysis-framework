## Original Question

Goodness (善), that is, goodness, has two dimensions. "ⓐPrimary goodness" refers to the essential perfection that a being possesses. This is an essential goodness in the sense that if any being lacks it, it can no longer be that being. In this sense, primary goodness signifies, in an active sense, the negation of privation. If a human lacks humanity, they are no longer human. As long as a human exists as a human, regardless of the differences in physical abilities or virtues among individuals, the assertion that someone is "more human" than others does not stand. Secondary goodness refers to the relationship between a subject and the accidental properties it possesses, as the property of "upright walking" pertains to being human. This kind of goodness is not what constitutes "being," but rather accompanies "being." Secondary goodness is subdivided into two. "ⓑThe first type of secondary goodness" refers to the suitability in which a property, in and of itself, contributes to the perfection of the being it belongs to. Health is not a primary goodness to a human, but a secondary one, because even a sick person is still human. And if health is good for a human, then health itself is a good property for humans. "ⓒThe second type of secondary goodness" refers to the goodness that is found in a being when a certain property belongs to it. In this sense of goodness, it is not the particular property itself that is good, but the overall goodness possessed by the being.
