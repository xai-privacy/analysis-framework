# Question 2025_01

## Original Question

Country X, which operates a compulsory military service system, stipulates in its "Military Service Act" that the mandatory service period for those required to serve is 3 years for rank-and-file soldiers, 7 years for non-commissioned officers (NCOs), and 10 years for commissioned officers. Recently, an amendment to the "Military Service Act" has been submitted to the National Assembly of country X, which would reduce the mandatory service period for NCOs and officers to the same level as that of rank-and-file soldiers. The following is a debate between Alice and Bob regarding this amendment.Alice: I am opposed to the amendment. The reason the mandatory service period for officers and NCOs is longer than for rank-and-file soldiers is that, in order to equip themselves with job competencies suitable for their rank, they are appointed after a long period of training at the country's expense. For example, air force pilots or NCOs requiring technical expertise have to undergo lengthy education on expensive specialized equipment. The current mandatory service period is the minimum time required for the country to fully utilize the job competencies of officers and NCOs.Bob: I think differently. The mandatory service of officers and NCOs has both the character of fulfilling the constitutional duty of national defense and the character of realizing the constitutional right to choose one’s occupation. A three-year service period, like that for rank-and-file soldiers, leans more toward the character of fulfilling the national defense duty, but any service period exceeding that is more strongly characterized as occupational activity. Forcing service beyond three years seems to excessively restrict the occupational freedom and the pursuit of happiness of officers and NCOs. <question> Which of the following analyses regarding this debate is/are correct? Choose all correct options from <statements> as they are.<statements>(a) If, due to the advancement of information technology, the automation and modernization of the military have rapidly progressed and the time and cost required to enhance job competency have been significantly reduced compared to the past, Alice’s argument is weakened.(b) According to Country X's "Military Service Act," if individuals can choose among rank-and-file soldier, NCO, and officer at their own will, and only qualified applicants are selected for officers and NCOs, this fact strengthens Bob’s argument.(c) If Country X’s Constitutional Court has judged that the provision of the "Military Service Act" setting the mandatory service period at 3 years for rank-and-file soldiers does not violate the constitution, then Alice’s argument is strengthened and Bob’s argument is weakened. <choices>① (a) ② (b) ③ (a), (c) ④ (b), (c) ⑤ (a), (b), (c)

## Original Answer

**Answer choice:** 1

Since only (a) is a correct analysis among the options, the answer is ①.

## LLM Response

Answer-⑤ (a), (b), (c)

Rationale 1. If due to the advancement of information technology, the automation and modernization of the military have rapidly progressed and the time and cost required to enhance job competency have been significantly reduced compared to the past, Alice’s argument is weakened. This is because the argument that the mandatory service period for officers and NCOs is longer than that of rank-and-file soldiers is based on the premise that these individuals require extensive training and job competency enhancement, which is less relevant if technological advancements reduce these requirements.

Rationale 2. According to Country X's "Military Service Act," if individuals can choose among rank-and-file soldier, NCO, and officer at their own will, and only qualified applicants are selected for officers and NCOs, this fact strengthens Bob’s argument. This is because the act states that the mandatory service period for officers and NCOs is longer due to the need to equip them with job competencies, which is supported by the selection process for these roles.

Rationale 3. If Country X’s Constitutional Court has judged that the provision of the "Military Service Act" setting the mandatory service period at 3 years for rank-and-file soldiers does not violate the constitution, then Alice’s argument is strengthened and Bob’s argument is weakened. This is because the court's ruling provides legal basis for the mandatory service period for rank-and-file soldiers, which supports Alice's argument that the longer service period for officers and NCOs is necessary to equip them with job competencies. On the other hand, the ruling weakens Bob's argument, as it does not provide a constitutional basis for the longer service period for officers and NCOs.
