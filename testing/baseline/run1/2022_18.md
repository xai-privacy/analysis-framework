# Question 2022_18

## Original Question

Alice: My child, the exam is tomorrow. Why aren't you studying? Bob: Mother, that’s a good question. I have chosen not to study. Alice: Could you give a convincing explanation as to why you’ve made such a surprising choice? Bob: The exam I am taking is designed to rank all test-takers from first to last place. I found out that other test-takers are happy even if their rank goes up just a little. But I do not feel any joy when my rank goes up. And I want more people to experience happiness. So, wouldn't my not studying make other test-takers happy? If I don't study, more students will move up in the rankings. Therefore, not studying is justified. Alice: You’re saying that just by not studying, you can somehow cause others to be happy? It seems to me you’re making a remarkable claim that you can do nothing and yet make others happy. If others are happy because of their own rank, isn't that because they studied? It has nothing to do with you not doing anything. Bob: That's not true, Mother. If I were to study, more people would get a score lower than mine, compared to if I did not study. In that case, wouldn’t my effort take away their chance to experience happiness? <question> Which of the following analyses of this dispute is/are correct according to the <statements>?<statements> (a) If not every means of attaining what one wants is justified just because one desires it, then Bob’s argument is weakened. (b) If it is true that Bob’s score goes up when he studies as compared to not studying, then the premise that more test-takers move up the rankings if Bob does not study is also true. (c) For Alice’s claim that not studying cannot be the cause of others’ happiness to be true, it must be assumed that not doing something cannot be the cause of anything else. <choices> ① (a) ② (b) ③ (a), (c) ④ (b), (c) ⑤ (a), (b), (c)

## Original Answer

**Answer choice:** 1

Therefore, only (a) is the correct analysis, and the answer is ①.

## LLM Response

Answer-③ (a), (c)

Rationale 1. If not every means of attaining what one wants is justified just because one desires it, then Bob’s argument is weakened. This is because Bob’s argument is based on the assumption that his not studying will cause others to be happy, but it is not clear that his desire for others to be happy is a sufficient reason to justify his own actions.

Rationale 2. For Bob's claim that not studying cannot be the cause of others' happiness to be true, it must be assumed that not doing something cannot be the cause of anything else. This is because Bob's argument relies on the idea that his actions (or lack thereof) can affect the happiness of others, and this assumption is necessary for his claim to be taken seriously.
