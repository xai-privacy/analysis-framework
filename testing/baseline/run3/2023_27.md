# Question 2023_27

## Original Question

There is a claim that the birth rate will decrease due to COVID-19, which began spreading in January 2020. The grounds for this claim are as follows. First, strong social distancing measures have made it relatively difficult for people to freely meet and date. Because of significant restrictions on using public facilities and traveling abroad, many people are postponing or giving up on marriage. Second, especially among low-income groups and young people—who generally have higher birth rates—unemployment rates have risen and income has decreased, leading to increased economic hardship. Since the economic burden of childbirth and childrearing is high, a decrease in income will result in a decrease in births. Third, as remote work and telecommuting have become widespread, the boundary between work and home has become blurred, causing many workers to complain of stress. Furthermore, with childcare facilities such as schools, kindergartens, and daycare centers being closed or partially operated, raising children has become even more difficult. Because of these childcare and childrearing challenges, not only families with young children but also those without children are likely to postpone or give up on having children. <question> Which of the following evaluations of the above argument are correct? Select all correct answers from the <statements> as they are. <statements> (a) If the total number of infants and young children decreased from July 2019 to July 2022, but there was no difference in the population under one year old, the argument is strengthened. (b) If the per capita Gross Domestic Product (GDP) in 2019 was 31,929,000 KRW and in 2020 it was 31,637,000 KRW, showing little difference, the argument is weakened. (c) If in August 2019, the percentage of women—both economically active and inactive—who reported being currently pregnant was 10% for both groups, and this percentage showed almost no change in the same survey conducted in August 2021, the argument is weakened. <choices> ① (b) ② (c) ③ (a), (b) ④ (a), (c) ⑤ (a), (b), (c)

## Original Answer

**Answer choice:** 2

Only (c) is a correct evaluation, so the correct answer is ②.

## LLM Response

Answer-⑤ (a), (b), (c)

Rationale 1. The claim that the birth rate will decrease due to COVID-19 is supported by several factors. Firstly, the social distancing measures have made it difficult for people to freely meet and date, leading to a postponement or abandonment of marriages. Secondly, the rise in unemployment rates and decreased income among low-income groups and young people, who generally have higher birth rates, has resulted in economic hardship, which can lead to a decrease in births. Lastly, the blurred boundary between work and home due to remote work and telecommuting, along with the closure or partial operation of childcare facilities, has made childcare and childrearing even more challenging, leading to a postponement or abandonment of having children.

Rationale 2. Option (a) is correct because if the total number of infants and young children decreased from July 2019 to July 2022, but there was no difference in the population under one year old, it would strengthen the argument. This is because the decline in the number of infants and young children would be attributed to the decrease in births, further supporting the claim that the COVID-19 pandemic has led to a decrease in the birth rate.

Rationale 3. Option (b) is correct because if the per capita GDP in 2019 was 31,929,000 KRW and in 2020 it was 31,637,000 KRW, showing little difference, it would weaken the argument. A decrease in per capita GDP would likely lead to an increase in birth rates, as couples may feel that they have more financial security to have children. Therefore, if the per capita GDP showed little difference between 2019 and 2020, it would weaken the argument that the COVID-19 pandemic has led to a decrease in birth rates.

Rationale 4. Option (c) is correct because if the percentage of women who reported being currently pregnant remained almost the same between August 2019 and August 2021, it would weaken the argument. A decline in the percentage of women who reported being pregnant would be expected if the COVID-19 pandemic had led to a decrease in birth rates. Therefore, if the percentage remained unchanged, it would weaken the argument that the COVID-19 pandemic has led to a decrease in the birth rate.
