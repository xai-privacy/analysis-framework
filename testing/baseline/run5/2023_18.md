# Question 2023_18

## Original Question

Alice: In the novel "A Study in Scarlet," the statement "Holmes is a detective" appears explicitly, so the proposition <Holmes is a detective> is explicitly true in this novel. However, nowhere in "A Study in Scarlet" is there any explicit statement about the number of Holmes’s nostrils. Nevertheless, it is common knowledge that Holmes is a human within the work, and humans generally have two nostrils, so a proposition such as <Holmes has two nostrils> would also be true in "A Study in Scarlet." In fact, although not expressed explicitly, there are many propositions that are implicitly true in "A Study in Scarlet," such as <The earth is round> and <All humans die>. Bob: Accepting that there are propositions that are implicitly true in fiction leads to unreasonable consequences. First, let’s note that sequels to fictional works can exist. Sequels have the power to determine the truth of things not made explicit in the original work. For example, in the novel "The Hobbit," it is not specified what kind of ring Bilbo possesses, but in its sequels, "The Lord of the Rings" series, it is made explicit that the ring is the One Ring. In this case, it is reasonable to regard the proposition that Bilbo’s ring is the One Ring as true even in "The Hobbit." Now, suppose the following. Conan Doyle writes a sequel to "A Study in Scarlet" titled "A Study in Red," and in that novel there is an explicit statement: "In fact, Holmes, who had had three nostrils since birth, was able to smell very well." Then it would also be reasonable to see the proposition <Holmes has three nostrils> as explicitly true not only in "A Study in Red" but also in "A Study in Scarlet." However, if <Holmes has two nostrils> is implicitly true in "A Study in Scarlet," then Holmes must have both two and three nostrils at the same time in "A Study in Scarlet." This is clearly an unreasonable consequence. Therefore, one can conclude that there is no such thing as implicit truth in fiction in addition to explicit truth. <question> Which of the following analyses of the above debate is/are correct? ## Original Answer

**Answer choice:** 2

Only (c) is a correct analysis, so the answer is ②.

## LLM Response

Answer-{⑤ (a), (b), (c)}

Rationale 1. Alice assumes that any proposition is either explicitly or implicitly true in a particular fiction, which is a reasonable assumption given the context of the discussion.

Rationale 2. Bob claims that if a proposition is explicitly stated in a sequel, it can retroactively make the proposition true in the original work. However, this does not necessarily mean that the original proposition becomes true, only that it becomes explicitly stated. The truth of the proposition remains dependent on the original work, not the sequel.
