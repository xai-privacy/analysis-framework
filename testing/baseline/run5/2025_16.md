# Question 2025_16

## Original Question

When someone is in trouble and needs help on the street, some passersby engage in prosocial behavior and help the person, while others just walk by. In order to predict whether a passerby will help, what should we identify? Should we know whether the passerby is generous or cold-hearted? Or should we identify external factors apart from personality traits? In order to investigate this, Alice, a psychologist, conducted the following experiment. Alice made 50% of the participants feel good by letting them smell the scent of fresh bread while walking, whereas the other 50% were not exposed to the scent. Immediately after, an assistant playing the role of a passerby dropped a file folder in front of each participant. As a result, 87.5% of the people who smelled the bread helped the passerby, while only 4% of those who did not smell the bread helped. Based on this, Alice came to the following conclusion: Regardless of personality, whether or not a person smelled the bread determines their behavior. In other words, the basis for predicting people’s behavior lies in situational factors, not personality traits. To explain this mechanism, Alice established the following hypothesis: ⓐ “The decisive factor influencing a person’s behavior is not personality but situational factors.” According to this hypothesis, the situational factor of having smelled fresh bread elevated the participants’ moods, which led to a motivation to help others. A small and unexpected good fortune made the person more generous. Based on the above experiment, Alice argued that there is a correlation between ‘prosocial behavior’ and ‘situational factors’. It is surprising that situational factors, rather than personality, determine behavior, but what is even more remarkable is ⓑ that “the factor inducing prosocial behavior can be very trivial or insignificant.” <question> Based on the above passage, which of the following evaluations regarding ⓐ and ⓑ are correct? Select all correct options from <statements>. <statements> (a) If it is found that most people who did not help the passerby were also known to behave selfishly in daily life, ⓐ is strengthened. (b) If it is found that the majority of the participants in Alice’s experiment were known to have altruistic personalities compared to those who were not, ⓐ is weakened. (c) If instead of exposing participants to the smell of bread, 50% of them were allowed to win a valuable prize and the other 50% were not, and the proportion of people who helped was similar to the bread smell experiment, then ⓐ is strengthened, but ⓑ is not weakened. <choices> ① (a) ② (c) ③ (a), (b) ④ (b), (c) ⑤ (a), (b), (c)

## Original Answer

**Answer choice:** 2

Since only (c) correctly evaluates the statements, the answer is ②.

## LLM Response

Answer-{③}.

Rationale 1. The passage states that the participants smelled the scent of fresh bread, which led to a positive mood, which in turn motivated them to help others. This suggests that the factor inducing prosocial behavior (the smell of bread) is trivial or insignificant, as a small good fortune (winning a valuable prize) would not have the same effect.

Rationale 2. The passage does not provide any information about the daily behavior of the participants outside the experiment. Therefore, it is not possible to make any conclusions about the participants' personalities based on their behavior in the experiment.
