# Question 2025_13

## Original Question

Alice and Bob each devised a meticulous plan with malicious intent to murder someone and attempted to carry it out. Alice succeeded in committing the murder, while Bob failed. In such a case, Alice is punished far more severely. Can this be justified? There are the following <views> on this matter. <View> A: Even though both Alice and Bob had the intention to kill, the fact that Alice's attempt succeeded and Bob's failed was due to 'luck.' It cannot be justified to impose different degrees of punishment on the two based on outcomes determined by luck, which is beyond their own control. This is because doing so does not treat them equally. Therefore, Alice and Bob should not be punished differently. B: The severity of punishment should depend on how malicious the criminal's intent was, but it is difficult to determine this precisely. In this situation, whether the murder succeeded is a good indicator of the malice of the intent. The more malicious the intent, the more meticulously the murder will be planned, and thus the greater the likelihood of success. Therefore, a successful murder should be punished more severely than a failed attempt. C: Punishing Alice more severely than Bob can be considered 'punishment by luck,' and such punishment can be a way to realize equal treatment. For example, in ancient times, when punishing traitors, only a few 'unlucky' people were punished through random drawing (lottery). Since the decision to punish was made for all traitors by giving each the same odds in the draw, one could say that they were treated equally. Similarly, punishing Alice and Bob differently based on the 'lottery' of murder success is also a way to realize equal treatment. <question> Among the following <statements>, which are correct analyses of the <views> above? <statements> (a) According to A, someone who had no intention to kill but killed a pedestrian through reckless driving, and someone who intended to kill and injured a pedestrian through reckless driving, should be punished the same. (b) If it is true both that the more malicious the intent, the more one is swayed by emotion and thus has poorer judgment, and that poorer judgment lowers the likelihood of a successful plan, then B's position is weakened. (c) A and C both agree that Alice and Bob should be treated equally in terms of punishment, but hold different opinions about what the punishment should be. <choices> ① (a) ② (b) ③ (a), (c) ④ (b), (c) ⑤ (a), (b), (c)

## Original Answer

**Answer choice:** 4

Only (b) and (c) are correct analyses, so the answer is ④.

## LLM Response

Answer-④

Rationale 1. A is correct because it implies that the punishment should be the same for someone who accidentally kills a pedestrian through reckless driving as for someone who intentionally kills a pedestrian through reckless driving. This is because the former is outside of their control, while the latter is a deliberate act.

Rationale 2. B is correct because it highlights the difficulty in determining the level of malice behind the intent. The position that the success of the murder is a good indicator of the malice of the intent is weakened by the fact that poorer judgment, which is related to emotion and motivation, affects the likelihood of a successful plan.

Rationale 3. A and C both agree that Alice and Bob should be treated equally in terms of punishment, but they hold different opinions about what the punishment should be. This indicates that while the views differ in the justification for punishing Alice more severely than Bob, both views agree that equal treatment is a principle.
