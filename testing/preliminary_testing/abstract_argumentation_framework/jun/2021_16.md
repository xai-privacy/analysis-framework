**Statements**

> (a) Bob would oppose the claim that the group of people who can perceive elegance could be larger or smaller depending on period and culture.
>
> (b) Charlie would oppose the claim that people who find Chopin's nocturnes boring may have a different kind of musical sensitivity.
>
> (c) Bob and Charlie can both accept the claim that Chopin's nocturnes are elegant, albeit for different reasons.

## Original Rationale

**Preliminaries**

This question concerns a debate over whether aesthetic properties—using the example of "elegance"—are real properties belonging to an object such as Chopin's nocturnes, or not. Alice's view is that aesthetic properties are not real properties of an object, and that attributing them is merely an expression of individual subjective impressions. Bob, in contrast, argues that aesthetic properties are real properties of the object, which can be perceived by listeners possessing the appropriate musical sensitivity under normal conditions. Meanwhile, Charlie contends that, although aesthetic properties are not simply a matter of individual subjective impressions, they are not genuine properties of the object either, and should be understood as being perceived differently depending on the shared musical sensitivity within each group. The question asks you to clearly recognize these differences in perspectives and to deduce their respective implications.

**Statement (a) Rationale**

(a) Bob claims that only those with appropriate musical sensitivity can perceive the elegance of the musical work. Since this means only that the group who can perceive elegance is limited to those with appropriate musical sensitivity, it is compatible with the idea that the size of this group could be larger or smaller depending on the era or culture. Thus, saying Bob would oppose this claim is an incorrect analysis.

**Statement (b) Rationale**

(b) Charlie maintains that people react differently to Chopin's nocturnes because the shared musical sensitivity of the groups they belong to is different. According to this explanation, people who find the nocturnes boring would indeed have musical sensitivities different from those who find them elegant. Therefore, (b) is also incorrect.

**Statement (c) Rationale**

(c) For Bob, only those with suitable musical sensitivity can perceive the genuine property of elegance in Chopin's nocturnes, so he can accept statements by people with such sensitivity that "Chopin's nocturnes are elegant." Meanwhile, for Charlie, even if elegance need not be a real property of Chopin's nocturnes, it is enough if there is a group sharing the relevant musical sensitivity who feel the music is elegant; thus, Charlie too can accept a group member's assertion that "Chopin's nocturnes are elegant." Therefore, (c) is a correct analysis.

**Answer**

Since only (c) is a correct analysis, the answer is ②.
