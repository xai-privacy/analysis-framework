# Question 2024_29

## Original Question

Alice: Economic behavior is the action of independent and rational individuals pursuing their own self-interest. The foundation of the ideal market, where perfect competition and self-regulation occur, is precisely the behavior of such atomized and rational people. Social relationships among people only hinder the competitive market.Bob: But, in reality, the market doesn’t function so perfectly. Coercion and deception also occur in the market. Of course, even coercion and deception arise from the pursuit of self-interest. Can the market really exert enough self-regulation to suppress people’s coercion and deception? At the very least, a certain degree of trust among people must be present.Charlie: The source of such trust is generalized morality. There exists an implicit agreement that one must respect others, and people mostly follow that agreement automatically. Humans naturally obey the commands of the norms and value systems we agree upon, because these are thoroughly internalized through socialization. Because morality is strongly shared, orderly transactions are guaranteed.Bob: However, in reality, it’s rare to find transaction situations where we can confirm that generalized morality is at work and that economic behavior is based on trust. To explain the general existence of orderly transactions in the market, we must pay attention to the fact that the intentional actions of agents are rooted in their current, concrete social relationships. What fosters trust and suppresses wrongdoing in the market are these specific private relationships and their networks. We prefer to trade with people who have a good reputation, not simply relying on generalized morality. Moreover, rather than depending only on general reputation, we try to gather information from acquaintances who know the trading partner well. Of course, we also try to maintain our own good reputation. The assumption of atomized individuals cannot explain reality. <question> Which of the following analyses of the above debate is/are correct according to the statements in <statements>? <statements> (a). Both Alice and Bob assume individuals who pursue their own self-interest. (b). Alice does not agree, but Bob does agree, with the claim that the social nature of relationships, which naturally develop as economic relations are sustained, is positive for economic life. (c). The concrete social relationships in which individuals are involved at the time of decision-making are more important to Bob than to Charlie. <choices> ① (a) ② (b) ③ (a), (c) ④ (b), (c) ⑤ (a), (b), (c)

## Original Answer

**Answer choice:** 5

Since (a), (b), and (c) are all correct, the answer is ⑤.

## LLM Response

Answer-③

Rationale 1. Although Alice assumes that the market operates based on individual self-interest, Bob accepts this assumption. Bob focuses on whether the self-regulation of the market is sufficient to eliminate coercion and deception, and whether trust among people is necessary.

Rationale 2. Charlie argues that the existence of trust is based on generalized morality, which is not supported by the statements. Charlie claims that humans obey norms and value systems through socialization, but the statements do not support this claim.

Rationale 3. Bob emphasizes the importance of concrete social relationships in fostering trust and suppressing wrongdoing, which is not supported by Charlie's statement. Bob believes that individuals try to gather information from acquaintances who know the trading partner well, which is not mentioned in Charlie's statement.
