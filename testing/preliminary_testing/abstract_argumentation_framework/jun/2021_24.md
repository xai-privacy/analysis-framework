## Question

> The roles of nature and culture in human development are a matter of debate, with opinions divided as follows.\<Opinions\>Alice: I believe that in order to understand human development, we must start from human nature. This is because there are clear differences between groups, and especially biological characteristics play a decisive role in explaining intergroup differences. In addition, many researchers have proven that differences in intelligence quotient between individuals are due to genetic factors.Bob: The fact that intelligence quotients among groups divided by race, gender, etc. show almost no difference is an undeniable fact accepted in the scientific community. Because the human species is so homogeneous, I think that insisting on intergroup differences based on biological criteria stems from a misguided motivation to uphold unequal social hierarchies. Social hierarchy only limits human variability; humans can be shaped differently depending on culture or social environment. \<question\> Which of the following in \<Statements\> properly evaluates the \<Opinions\>? \<statements\> (a) Research results indicating that in every society throughout history, crime rates have been overwhelmingly higher in a particular group—males between the ages of 15 and 25—weaken Alice's argument.(b) Research results that various social reforms aimed at fairly developing the abilities of all members of society have failed do not strengthen Bob's argument.(c) Research results showing that, as a result of improving English education programs, the English test scores of most elementary school students increased significantly compared to before the improvement, strengthen Alice's argument and weaken Bob's argument. \<choices\> ① (a) ② (b) ③ (a), (c) ④ (b), (c) ⑤ (a), (b), (c)

**Statements**

> (a) Research results indicating that in every society throughout history, crime rates have been overwhelmingly higher in a particular group—males between the ages of 15 and 25—weaken Alice's argument.
>
> (b) Research results that various social reforms aimed at fairly developing the abilities of all members of society have failed do not strengthen Bob's argument.
>
> (c) Research results showing that, as a result of improving English education programs, the English test scores of most elementary school students increased significantly compared to before the improvement, strengthen Alice's argument and weaken Bob's argument.

## Original Rationale

**Preliminaries**

In the \<Opinions\>, Alice emphasizes the importance of human nature, particularly biological characteristics, in the formation of humans. She also claims that these biological traits cause differences between individuals and groups. On the other hand, Bob emphasizes the importance of culture or social environment in human development. He argues that emphasizing biological traits amounts to supporting social hierarchy and rebutts by saying there are actually very few intergroup differences based on biological factors. He further stresses the potential for human development depending on cultural or social environmental changes.

**Statement (a) Rationale**

(a) The claim that, throughout history, in every society crime rates have been high in a specific group—males aged 15 to 25—means that in any culture or social environment, there is consistently a high crime rate among a particular age group of males. In other words, regardless of cultural or social environmental change, this research result, which suggests that the biological trait of being a male of a certain age greatly influences crime rates, actually strengthens Alice's argument emphasizing biological characteristics in human development, rather than weakening it. Thus, (a) is not an appropriate evaluation.

**Statement (b) Rationale**

(b) Various social reforms aimed at equally developing the abilities of all members of society can be understood as attempts to promote human development by changing culture or social environment. Therefore, the finding that such reforms failed does not strengthen Bob’s claim, which argues for the potential for human variability and development through environmental change. Thus, (b) is an appropriate evaluation.

**Statement (c) Rationale**

(c) The fact that, due to improvements in English education programs, most elementary school students’ test scores increased greatly compared to before the improvements shows that human development can be achieved through changes in environment, especially through changes in systems. Therefore, this research result weakens Alice's argument, which emphasizes biological characteristics in human development, while strengthening Bob’s argument, which emphasizes the importance of environment. Thus, (c) is not an appropriate evaluation.

**Answer**

Therefore, only (b) in the \<Choices\> is an appropriate evaluation, so the correct answer is ②.

Both approaches select ⑤ against a key of ②, and both carry the same underlying error, which is that Alice’s and Bob’s positions on the role of environment are swapped. The framework makes that error visible earlier, in the extracted arguments, where the baseline only shows it in the verdicts. That is useful for failure analysis and does nothing for accuracy. The attack graph here is the weakest of the five: it announces an edge it does not draw and adds two nodes with relations that are not attacks.
