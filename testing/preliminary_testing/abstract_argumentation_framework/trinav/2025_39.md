## Original Rationale

**Preliminaries**

The biological species concept—that a species is a group of individuals that are reproductively isolated from other groups and can interbreed within the group—is commonly used, but as a complete definition, it has many problems. For example, if A and B can interbreed, and B and C can interbreed, but A and C cannot, then A and B are the same species, and B and C are the same species, so A and C should also be the same species; however, since A and C can't interbreed, this situation shows that A and C are not the same species. Thus, species formation according to Darwin's theory of evolution based on biological species concept has several issues. The passage explains, using plants that can develop normally even in abnormal polyploid states due to errors in meiosis and fertilization, that apart from speciation by Darwinian evolution, new synthetic species can also arise between different species—Dr. Woo's 'Woo's Triangle Model'.

**Statement (a) Rationale**

According to Woo's Triangle Model, through natural hybridization between two different species within the genus Brassica, the chromosomes of one species are added to those of another, resulting in a new species that possesses both sets. Therefore, for Ethiopian mustard, a synthetic species from black mustard (n=8) and cabbage (n=9), the number of chromosomes (n) would be 17. Thus, the number of chromosomes (n=17) in Ethiopian mustard is greater than that in black mustard (n=8), so (a) is correct.

**Statement (b) Rationale**

According to ⓐ, a species is defined as a group of populations that can interbreed. As it has been confirmed that Chinese cabbage and cabbage can interbreed, they cannot be considered different species. Also, according to ⓑ, a new species is created by natural hybridization between two different species in the genus Brassica; since black mustard and Chinese cabbage produce leaf mustard, black mustard and leaf mustard can be considered different species. Thus, (b) is correct.

**Statement (c) Rationale**

According to ⓐ, the Maltese (n=39) and Poodle (n=39) are classified as the same species, and since a Maltipoo (n=39) born from them has no change in chromosome number, Maltese, Poodle, and Maltipoo should all be considered the same species. Since Woo's Triangle Model (ⓑ) applies to cases where, by natural hybridization, a new species is created that possesses both sets of chromosomes from different species, it does not apply to these dogs, whose chromosome numbers are all the same. Therefore, the statement that the theory of species synthesis used in ⓑ would classify the Poodle and the Maltipoo as different species is incorrect. (c) is not a correct analysis.

**Answer**

Therefore, only (a) and (b) are correct, so the answer is ③.

> According to the rationale, statements (a) and (b) are correct.
