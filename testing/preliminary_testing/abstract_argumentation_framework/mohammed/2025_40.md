## 2. LEET-Arg Question

```text
Hormones that are synthesized in one tissue, move to another tissue, and regulate physiological activity in that tissue can be classified into peptide hormones and steroid hormones. Most steroid hormones have low polarity and easily penetrate the cell membrane to act directly as signaling molecules inside the cell, whereas most peptide hormones have high polarity and cannot penetrate the cell membrane. Therefore, peptide hormones that cannot penetrate the cell membrane bind to receptors present on the cell surface and use a mechanism that activates intracellular signaling proteins through structural changes in these receptors. Animal steroid hormones penetrate the cell membrane easily, but the plant steroid hormone brassinolide (Br), which promotes plant growth, is known to be unable to penetrate the cell membrane easily, despite there being no significant difference between the cell membranes of animal cells and plant cells. <Experiment> is part of a study that elucidated the mechanism by which Br, similar to peptide hormones, utilizes cell surface receptors and intracellular signaling proteins. <Experiment> Among Arabidopsis mutants, mutant A with a defect only in the Br biosynthesis gene and mutant B with a defect only in the Br receptor gene were isolated. Both showed a growth-inhibited phenotype compared to the wild type. When Br was applied, the inhibited growth of A was not restored, but B’s growth was restored. A mutant C, which exhibited a growth-inhibited phenotype due solely to a mutation in the gene for the signaling protein that recognizes the structural change in the Br receptor, was also selected, and the phenotypic change after Br treatment was observed. <question> Which of the following analyses of the above passage is/are correct? Select all correct statements among those listed in <statements>: <statements> (a) If the difference in membrane permeability between animal and plant steroid hormones is determined only by polarity, Br would be more polar than animal steroid hormones. (b) <Experiment> supports the hypothesis that “A cannot synthesize Br, and B has a defect in the Br receptor.” (c) After Br treatment, B and C will show similar phenotypes. <choices> ① (a) ② (c) ③ (a), (b) ④ (b), (c) ⑤ (a), (b), (c)
```

## 3. Gold Answer and Original Rationale

```text
Gold answer: 1
```

**preliminaries:** Due to differences in polarity, steroid hormones can freely pass through the cell membrane and act directly inside the cell, whereas peptide hormones, which cannot cross the membrane, transmit signals into the cell through surface receptors. The plant hormone brassinolide (BR) has recently been shown to use cell surface receptors and intracellular signaling proteins like peptide hormones because, even though it is a steroid hormone, it cannot cross the cell membrane. Part of an experiment elucidating this has been introduced.

**statement_1_rationale:** (a) In the passage, it is stated, "Most steroid hormones have low polarity and therefore easily penetrate the cell membrane and act directly as signaling molecules inside the cell, whereas most peptide hormones have high polarity and cannot penetrate the cell membrane." From this, we can deduce that higher polarity means lower membrane permeability. It is also stated, "Animal steroid hormones can easily cross the cell membrane, but the plant steroid hormone brassinolide (BR) ... is known not to be able to cross the membrane easily." Therefore, "If the difference in membrane permeability between animal and plant steroid hormones is determined only by polarity, then BR would be more polar than animal steroid hormones" is a correct analysis.

**statement_2_rationale:** (b) In <Experiment>, since B’s inhibited growth recovered after Br treatment, we can infer that B has a mutation in the Br biosynthesis gene. Since A’s inhibited growth was not restored, we can infer that A has a mutation in the Br receptor gene. Therefore, the experiment does NOT support the hypothesis that “A cannot synthesize Br, and B has a defect in the Br receptor.” Thus, (b) is not a correct analysis.

**statement_3_rationale:** (c) C shows a growth-inhibited phenotype solely due to a mutation in the signaling protein gene, so BR is being synthesized normally. Therefore, simply applying BR cannot restore C’s inhibited growth, so it will show a similar phenotype to A, for whom growth does not recover with BR application. Therefore, "After Br treatment, B and C will show similar phenotypes" is not a correct analysis.

**answer:** Only (a) is correct, so the answer is ①.

## 14. Argumentation Framework Result Analysis

- **Predicted answer:** 2 / statement (c), inferred from the model selecting statement_3 as the only clearly correct statement
- **Gold answer:** 1
- **Correct or incorrect:** Incorrect
- **Did argument extraction help?** Only partially. The extraction captured the broad idea that Br behaves more like peptide hormones by using a surface-receptor/signaling pathway, but it did not preserve the key intervention logic of the experiment. In particular, it treated the A/B mutant observations as general support for the pathway rather than using Br restoration/non-restoration to infer which mutation is upstream versus receptor/signaling-side.
- **Did attack-relation extraction help?** No. The attack-relation step converted causal/experimental relationships into a vague implication chain (`A → B → C`) and explicitly admitted these were implications rather than attacks. This artificial structure did not help the final reasoning.
- **Main remaining issue:** The model reversed both the polarity logic and the mutant-treatment logic. It said Br would be less polar than animal steroid hormones, when the passage implies that lower permeability corresponds to higher polarity. It also treated B and C as similar after Br treatment, although B’s growth is restored while C should remain growth-inhibited because its downstream signaling protein is defective.

- **Baseline outcome:** Incorrect; the baseline selected ④ / statements (b) and (c), while the gold answer is ① / statement (a).
- **Argumentation-framework outcome:** Incorrect; the argumentation-framework run selected statement (c) rather than the gold answer ① / statement (a).
- **Improved / worse / inconclusive:** Did not improve. Both approaches failed, and the argumentation-framework version did not repair the baseline’s misunderstanding of polarity or mutant-response logic.

Both runs failed on the same central requirement: translating the biological experiment into the correct causal interpretation. The baseline rejected the correct polarity statement and selected the wrong option combination. The argumentation-framework approach extracted some relevant mechanism information, but the attack graph was not useful because this item is not primarily a speaker-attack debate; it is an experimental causal-reasoning problem. The intermediate structure therefore added complexity without improving the final answer.

## 16. Final Notes

This question shows that the three-step argumentation prompt is not naturally suited to every LEET-Arg item. The item is less of a speaker-disagreement problem and more of a causal experimental-reasoning problem. The model struggled because it forced the passage into an argument/attack format even though the crucial reasoning is about intervention effects: Br treatment restores a biosynthesis mutant but not a receptor/signaling mutant. A better prompt for this category may need to ask the model to extract variables, interventions, expected phenotypes, and causal dependencies before evaluating the answer statements.
