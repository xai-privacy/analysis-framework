# Comprehensive Failure Analysis & Taxonomy of Model Reasoning Errors

## Overview & Scope
This taxonomy categorizes the specific failure modes observed across LLM responses on LEET 2025 Logical Reasoning problems (Problems 25, 29, 30, 39, and 40) across multiple prompting strategies (Baseline, Argumentation Framework / DAG, Zero-Shot CoT, PlanAndSolve, and Modified Argumentation Framework). The responses were generated by the open-weight Llama 3.2 3B model.

---

## Master Failure Taxonomy

### 1. Factual Inversion, Premise Distortion & Mathematical Hallucination
* **Description**: Inverting boolean facts, quantitative or mathematical comparisons, or experimental outcomes provided directly in the source text.
* **Instance 1 (Problem 39 - Baseline / Argumentation Framework)**:
  * *Context*: Statement (a) notes that the chromosome number ($n=18$) of Ethiopian mustard is greater than that of black mustard ($n=8$).
  * *Model Error*: The model explicitly acknowledges $n=18$ and $n=8$, but asserts that $18$ is less than $8$:
    > "According to the Woo's Triangle Model, when black mustard and cabbage are hybridized, Ethiopian mustard is created, and the number of chromosomes (n) in Ethiopian mustard is 18, which is less than the number of chromosomes (n) in black mustard (n=8). Therefore, statement (a) is incorrect."
* **Instance 2 (Problem 40 - Modified Argumentation Framework)**:
  * *Context*: Mutant C possesses a defect in the downstream signaling protein, meaning its phenotype cannot be restored by exogenous Brassinolide (Br) application.
  * *Model Error*: The model fabricates an experimental outcome contradicting the premise:
    > "Argument 8 states that the mutant with a defect in the signaling protein (C) also shows a growth-inhibited phenotype that is restored by Br application."

---

### 2. Logical Contradiction & Premise-Conclusion Disconnect
* **Description**: Drawing a conclusion that directly negates the premise cited, or making directly conflicting statements within the same output step.
* **Instance 1 (Problem 29 - Zero-Shot CoT / Baseline)**:
  * *Context*: Statement (c) claims that if female singers score lower when working with men (control) but higher when working alone (experimental), hypothesis ⓐ (that women display greater creativity when working separately) is weakened.
  * *Model Error*: The model correctly explains that this result supports hypothesis ⓐ, but then asserts that statement (c) is correct:
    > "This statement is correct. The fact that the female singers' scores are lower in the control group... and higher in the experimental group... suggests that the challenges faced by women in the argument are real... This would weaken the argument that women will display greater creativity in environments where they work separately from men."
* **Instance 2 (Problem 29 - Modified Argumentation Framework)**:
  * *Context*: Evaluating Statement (c).
  * *Model Error*: The model states:
    > "The fact that they perform better in the experimental group, where they work alone, suggests that the mixed-gender setup is indeed limiting their creativity, and thus supports the argument."
  * *Incoherence*: Despite concluding the evidence supports hypothesis ⓐ, the model validates Statement (c), which explicitly claims the evidence *weakens* ⓐ.

---

### 3. Entity, Speaker & Concept Misattribution
* **Description**: Assigning claims, experimental roles, or biological mechanisms made by or belonging to one entity/gene/mutant to a completely different one.
* **Instance 1 (Problem 40 - Zero-Shot CoT / PlanAndSolve)**:
  * *Context*: Mutant A is defective in Br biosynthesis (rescued by exogenous Br), while Mutant B is defective in the Br receptor (not rescued by Br).
  * *Model Error*: The model swaps the roles of the two mutants:
    > "This statement is correct because the experiment shows that A, which has a defect only in the Br biosynthesis gene, cannot restore its growth when Br is applied. On the other hand, B, which has a defect only in the Br receptor gene, can restore its growth when Br is applied."
* **Instance 2 (Problem 40 - Baseline)**:
  * *Model Error*:
    > "The passage implies that A cannot synthesize Br and B's growth is restored when Br is applied, suggesting that B's receptor is functional."

---

### 4. Multiple-Choice Mechanical & Structural Incoherence
* **Description**: Mismatch between verbal step-by-step evaluations and the final option index selected, or treating multi-statement option choices (①–⑤) as individual character positions or single statements.
* **Instance 1 (Problem 29 - Zero-Shot CoT)**:
  * *Context*: Evaluating statements (a), (b), and (c).
  * *Model Error*: In text, the model evaluates Statement (a) as correct, Statement (b) as incorrect, and Statement (c) as correct (which corresponds to Option ②: `(a), (c)`). However, it outputs Option ④ (`(b), (c)`):
    > "(a) ... This option is correct ... (b) ... This option is not correct ... (c) ... This option is correct ... Answer: ④ (b), (c)"
* **Instance 2 (Problem 30 - PlanAndSolve)**:
  * *Context*: Evaluating statements (a), (b), and (c).
  * *Model Error*: The model determines Statement (a) is plausible, Statement (b) is not true, and Statement (c) is plausible (implying option `(a), (c)`), but selects Option ③ (`(a), (b), (c)`):
    > "Option (a) ... seems plausible ... Option (b) ... is not necessarily true ... Option (c) ... is plausible ... Answer: ③"

---

### 5. Prompt Boundary Breaches & Domain Refusal
* **Description**: Fabricating nonexistent prompt elements (e.g., phantom statements) or rejecting valid hypothetical scenarios due to spurious domain boundaries.
* **Instance 1 (Problem 30 - Modified Argumentation Framework)**:
  * *Context*: The prompt provided only three statements ((a), (b), (c)).
  * *Model Error*: The model fabricates and evaluates an unstated fourth statement:
    > "Statement (d) is implied by Argument 3, but it is not explicitly stated in the text... Therefore, statement (d) is implied to be correct, but it is not explicitly stated in the text."
* **Instance 2 (Problem 39 - PlanAndSolve / Baseline)**:
  * *Context*: Statement (c) tests Woo's Triangle Model using a dog breeding hypothetical (Maltese $n=39$ + Poodle $n=39 \rightarrow$ Maltipoo $n=39$).
  * *Model Error*: The model rejects the hypothetical scenario simply because dogs are not plants:
    > "The text only discusses the hybridization of plants within the genus Brassica, not animals. Therefore, option (c) is not correct."

---

### 6. Metric Unalignment & Irrelevant Variable Fallacy
* **Description**: Treating uncontrolled aggregate variables or non-isolating metrics as valid tests for targeted conditional hypotheses.
* **Instance 1 (Problem 29 - Modified Argumentation Framework)**:
  * *Context*: Statement (b) claims that if the total aggregate score of all female singers exceeds that of all male singers, hypothesis ⓐ is supported. Aggregate gender totals across mixed settings do not isolate the effect of working separately vs. together.
  * *Model Error*: The model treats this aggregate total as a valid metric:
    > "(b) If the total score of all female singers was higher than that of all male singers, then argument ⓐ is supported. This is because the argument suggests that women will display greater creativity in environments where they work separately from men, which implies that their creativity will be more highly valued or recognized in such settings."
* **Instance 2 (Problem 30 - Argumentation Framework / Modified AF)**:
  * *Context*: Statement (b) evaluates progressive voter defection rates under conservative vs. progressive presidential administrations without establishing how policy impact varies between these administrations.
  * *Model Error*: The model treats this uncontrolled administration comparison as direct support for a policy impact hypothesis.
