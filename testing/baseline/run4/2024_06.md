# Question 2024_06

## Original Question

An incident occurred in Country X where a drone was used to film the interior of a high-rise apartment living room without permission, raising the issue of whether "ⓐ the act of photographing the interior of another person's residence from outside without authorization" can be punished under [the Regulation]. [Regulation] Article 1 (Crime of Secret Surveillance): Anyone who records or listens to others’ undisclosed residence or building interiors by means such as recording or listening shall be punished by imprisonment for not more than five years. Article 2 (Crime of Illegal Search): Anyone who searches another person’s residence or building without authority shall be punished by imprisonment for not more than three years. <Opinions> A: ⓐ falls under the crime of secret surveillance. 'Surveillance' means ascertaining information about the interior of a residence, thereby infringing on the sense of security regarding privacy enjoyed by the resident; and 'by means such as recording or listening' should not necessarily be restricted to only auditory or acoustic methods but can include visual methods such as video recording or viewing. B: ⓐ falls under the crime of illegal search. 'Search' refers to examining a certain place in order to discover a person or object. Although the existing interpretation of illegal search crimes punished those who, having lawfully entered a residence or building, conducted a search without authority, the text of the illegal search crime itself is not limited only to when someone has entered the residence or building. Therefore, illegal search crimes should also cover cases where a person unlawfully enters or, even from outside, searches the interior without authority; thus, a person who conducts such a search from outside the residence or building can also be punished. <question> Considering the above <Opinions>, which of the following from <statements> are correct? <statements> (a). If the Country X court has ruled that merely peeking into another’s residence through the window from the outside with the naked eye does not constitute 'surveillance' under the crime of secret surveillance, then A is weakened. (b). If the legislative intent behind prescribing different penalties in Article 1 and Article 2 is that unlawfully entering another’s residence to obtain information is more wrongful than obtaining information by lawful entry, then B is strengthened. (c). If the Country X court has ruled that an act where the police, to find the wanted person, film Bob’s residence from outside using a drone without a warrant does not infringe on the sense of privacy but does constitute an unlawful “search,” then A is weakened and B is strengthened. <choices> ① (a) ② (b) ③ (a), (c) ④ (b), (c) ⑤ (a), (b), (c)

## Original Answer

**Answer choice:** 3

Since only (a) and (c) are correct evaluations, the answer is ③.

## LLM Response

Answer-⑤ (a), (b), (c)

Rationale 1. (a) is correct because if the court ruled that mere peeking into another's residence through the window without surveillance constitutes "surveillance," it would indeed weaken Opinion A. The court's ruling would imply that A's broad interpretation of "surveillance" as including visual methods like video recording or viewing is not necessary, given the fact that mere peeking doesn't constitute surveillance.

Rationale 2. (b) is correct because if the legislative intent behind prescribing different penalties in Article 1 and Article 2 is that unlawfully entering another's residence to obtain information is more wrongful than obtaining information by lawful entry, then it supports and strengthens Opinion B. This legislative intent would suggest that the crime of "secret surveillance" is specifically designed to target the act of obtaining information through unauthorized means, making B's interpretation of the crime more accurate.
