# Question 2021_18

## Original Question

A: 'Epistemic objectivity' is related not so much to whether a claim is true or false, but rather to how that claim is justified. In the process of making a claim, it is necessary to ensure that healthy common sense and rationality are not distorted by the intervention of subjective elements such as bias, prejudice, herd mentality, or personal wishes, whether they belong to oneself or anyone else. In this sense, a judgment that has secured epistemic objectivity generally possesses persuasive power.B: For a work of art to have its intended effect, it must be appreciated from a particular perspective. If the critic’s situation does not align with what the work demands, the critic cannot make an appropriate judgment about the work. For example, when an advocate delivers a speech to a specific audience, he or she must consider that audience’s unique characteristics, interests, views, passions, and prejudices. If a critic from another era or country encounters this speech, in order to make the correct judgment, the critic must take all these circumstances into account and place himself or herself in the same situation as the contemporary audience. The same applies to works of art. Even if the critic is a friend or an adversary of the artist, they must set aside their own particular circumstances and adopt the perspective presupposed by the work. <question> Which of the following analyses of the above passages is/are correct? Choose all correct answers from among the <statements> as given.<statements>(a) If two people make the same judgment about a claim, according to Alice, their judgment possesses epistemic objectivity.(b) According to Alice, the judgment that Bob’s critic makes about a work of art does not possess epistemic objectivity.(c) If two critics from different eras or countries make the same judgment about a given work of art, according to Bob, their judgment is made from the perspective presupposed by the work. <choices> ① (a) ② (b) ③ (a), (c) ④ (b), (c) ⑤ (a), (b), (c)

## Original Answer

**Answer choice:** 2

Only (b) among the <choices> is a correct analysis, so the answer is ②.
