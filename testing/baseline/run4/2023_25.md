## Original Question

According to functionalists, we have successfully understood the human mind by attributing mental states to others through folk psychology. Folk psychology refers to a theoretical system composed of mental terms such as “beliefs” and “desires.” For example, we predict that most drivers will stop at a red light. This is because most rational drivers believe that they should stop at a red light. Therefore, for functionalists, the existence of mental states is a matter of course. However, eliminativists argue that folk psychology should be discarded. Theories without scientific explanatory power and predictive ability have disappeared throughout history. In such cases, the entities assumed by those theories, as well as the terms referring to them, have also been eliminated. Generally, whether a theory is right or wrong depends on whether it successfully predicts given phenomena. But how often do we misunderstand others! In chemistry, with the complete failure of alchemy, the existence of the philosopher’s stone—which was once considered the seed of gold—was denied, and even the term “philosopher’s stone” disappeared. Similarly, not only should the existence of the mind postulated by failed theory be eliminated, but even mental terms such as “beliefs” and “desires” should also be removed. Instrumentalists argue that by positing the existence of mental states, we can predict behavior. Let’s consider the example of a chess-playing computer. Certainly, a computer does not have mental states like beliefs or desires. However, if we attribute beliefs or desires to the chess computer—such as “The computer believes it should capture the queen” or “The computer wants to save the king”—we can more efficiently predict the computer’s next move. In the same way, if we attribute mental states to humans in our explanations, this becomes a great help for predicting human behavior. Nevertheless, instrumentalists think that the mental entities we posit as tools do not actually exist in the mind. <question> Which of the statements in <statements> is/are correct as an analysis of the passage above? <statements> (a) Functionalists and instrumentalists hold different views about the existence of mental states, but they share the same view regarding the usefulness of mental terms. (b) Both eliminativists and instrumentalists acknowledge the necessity of mental terms. (c) Both eliminativists and instrumentalists present the same reason to support the claim that mental states do not exist. <choices> ① (a) ② (b) ③ (a), (c) ④ (b), (c) ⑤ (a), (b), (c)

## Original Answer

**Answer choice:** 1

Only (a) is a correct analysis, so the answer is ①.

## LLM Response

Answer-④ (b), (c)

 Rationale 1. The passage states that eliminativists discard folk psychology, which suggests that they do not acknowledge the necessity of mental terms, making option (b) incorrect. 

 Rationale 2. The passage states that instrumentalists argue that mental entities are tools that do not exist in the mind but are useful for prediction, making option (c) correct.
