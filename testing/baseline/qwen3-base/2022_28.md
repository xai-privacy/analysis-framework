## Original question

The Victim Impact Statement (VIS) system provides victims with an opportunity to express the impact they have suffered from a crime during the sentencing phase of a trial. There is a tendency for sentences to be heavier when a VIS is submitted compared to when it is not, and two views have been suggested regarding the reasons for this phenomenon. According to View A, it is not the presence of the VIS itself but the severity of the harm described in the VIS that leads to heavier sentences. According to this view, the more severe the harm, the heavier the sentence, as victims who have suffered more serious harm are more likely to submit a VIS in order to highlight the details of the harm described in the indictment, whereas victims who have not suffered serious harm tend not to submit a VIS. Therefore, it appears as though there is a meaningful relationship between VIS and sentencing. According to View B, not only are judges or jurors influenced by the harm described in the VIS, but they are also influenced by the strong emotions expressed by the victim through the VIS; as a result, the VIS itself induces a heavier sentence. Two research methods, P and Q, have been devised to test the validity of each view.P: Mock jurors, randomly selected, are divided into three groups and provided with objective information about a case.[Group 1] receives a VIS describing emotional injury that is much more serious than generally expected; [Group 2] receives a VIS describing emotional injury that is less serious than generally expected; [Group 3] receives no VIS. The average sentence given by each group is then compared.Q: Mock jurors, randomly selected, are divided into three groups and provided with objective information about a case.[Group 1] is presented with a video of a trial in which the victim reads a serious VIS in a highly emotional manner; [Group 2] is presented with a video of a trial in which the victim reads the same VIS in a calm manner; [Group 3] is presented with a video in which the victim calmly reads a VIS that is less serious than the previous one.
