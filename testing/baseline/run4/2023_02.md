# Question 2023_02

## Original Question

[Law A] Article 1: The Supreme Court is responsible for handling appeals, which are the final instance in the three-tier court system. Article 2: The Supreme Court may dismiss an appeal by judgment without holding a trial if it considers the grounds for the appeal to be inappropriate. Article 3: In a judgment dismissing the appeal pursuant to Article 2, the reasons for such dismissal may be omitted. <Argument> Article 2 of Law A is a provision designed to prevent abuse of the appeal process at the Supreme Court and to faithfully guarantee the right to trial for parties with proper grounds for appeal, through swift handling of cases. In light of the legislative intent and content of the regulation, it is sufficiently reasonable. Article 3 of Law A is a procedural provision required to realize Article 2. That is, requiring reasons to be stated in a judgment of dismissal would only impose unnecessary burdens on the Supreme Court and waste time that should be spent on cases that truly need a trial, so the intent of Article 3 is also justified. Generally, the purpose of requiring reasons in judgments is to allow parties to understand the court’s reasoning and to seek remedies if dissatisfied, but just as the reasons for judgment may be omitted in minor cases such as small claims for the sake of speedy processing, the stating of reasons is not an essential element of a judgment, but is something the court may choose at its discretion. Furthermore, the existence of the Supreme Court does not mean that every case must be guaranteed a trial by the Supreme Court. Therefore, the detailed content of Supreme Court procedures, including whether to state reasons in its judgments, falls within the Court’s discretion. <question> Among the <statements> below, which can serve as counterarguments to the <Argument>? <statements> (a) The right to a trial is the right to demand a judicial act by the state, and the right of claim necessarily involves a corresponding duty on the other party, which includes both the obligation to respond to the claim and the obligation to answer faithfully. (b) The right to a trial is characterized primarily as a right to guarantee access to procedures and procedural fairness, and the legitimacy of a court’s judgment depends on the presentation of grounds for that judgment. (c) The Supreme Court’s judgments serve as the de facto standard for interpreting similar cases and making normative evaluations among the public, and not only the conclusions of judgments but also their reasoning processes perform this function. <choices> ① (a) ② (b) ③ (a), (c) ④ (b), (c) ⑤ (a), (b), (c).

## Original Answer

**Answer choice:** 5

Since all of (a), (b), and (c) can serve as counterarguments to the <Argument>, the correct answer is ⑤.

## LLM Response

Answer-③. Rationale 1. Statement (c) addresses the broader implications of the Supreme Court's judgments, which can serve as counterarguments to the Argument by highlighting the importance of the court's reasoning processes in setting a standard for similar cases.
