# Question 2024_20

## Original Question

Alice: Humans interact with the world based on perception. However, sometimes humans perceive objects incorrectly. The ability to perceive in a way that accurately corresponds to the external world was important in human evolution, as it can make a difference in actual behavior. That's why distinguishing between accurate representation and misrepresentation is important.Bob: With perception alone, we cannot distinguish between accurate representation and misrepresentation. Visual perception is determined only by the information delivered to the retina. At this time, there may be multiple external objects corresponding to the same perception, but we cannot know which one is the real one. For example, a brownish-yellow surface is perceived as a neutral yellow when its surroundings are red, and a greenish-yellow surface is also perceived as neutral yellow when its surroundings are green. In these cases, we perceive only the neutral yellow and have no way of knowing what the actual color of the surface is.Alice: So, what you're saying is that it's difficult to tell the difference between an unripe and a ripe banana, right? If I can't know what I'm seeing, I can't pick and eat a ripe banana. This would be a disadvantage for humans in the course of evolution.Bob: Of course, it would be nice to be able to identify and eat only ripe ones. However, we don't need to know precisely at every moment whether it is unripe or fully ripe. There are more yellow bananas within our range of action. Supermarket shelves are full of yellow bananas. So if you eat based on the perception of yellow, you'll end up eating ripe bananas. <question> Which of the following analyses of the above debate are appropriate? Choose the correct ones from <statements> as they are.<statements>(a) Even if the perceiver can know which of the multiple objects producing the same perception most likely corresponds accurately to that perception, Alice's claim is not weakened.(b) If two circles of different sizes can be perceived as the same size depending on the size of the circles surrounding each, then Bob's claim is weakened.(c) Regarding the statement "Some perceptions correspond accurately to external objects," Alice does not agree but Bob does. <choices>① (a) ② (b) ③ (a), (c) ④ (b), (c) ⑤ (a), (b), (c)

## Original Answer

**Answer choice:** 1

Since only (a) is an appropriate analysis, the answer is ①.

## LLM Response

Answer-③

Rationale 1. (a) is incorrect because Alice's claim that distinguishing between accurate representation and misrepresentation is important is not weakened by knowing which of the multiple objects producing the same perception most likely corresponds accurately to that perception. The distinction remains between the perception and the external world.

Rationale 2. (b) is incorrect because the comparison of two circles of different sizes being perceived as the same size depending on the size of the circles surrounding each is not relevant to the discussion about distinguishing between accurate representation and misrepresentation.

Rationale 3. (c) is correct because Alice does not agree that some perceptions correspond accurately to external objects, while Bob does. In other words, Bob is suggesting that some perceptions are accurate, while Alice is questioning this.
