# Question 2023_14

## Original Question

Alice: It’s not physically possible to harm a dead person, but there are cases where people mistakenly think that something can still benefit or harm them. For example, viciously spreading false rumors about someone who has died does not actually harm them. However, it can still hurt the feelings of living people related to them, such as descendants or others who admired them. Bob: But why do living people feel hurt? It’s because they think the vicious rumour harmed the deceased as well. Suppose, for instance, a mother adamantly declared during her lifetime that she would never sell the house she lived in, and wished for it not to be sold even after her death. After the mother passes away, her daughter inherits the house and, following her mother's wishes, does not intend to sell it. The reason for this is that she believes selling it would go against her mother’s wishes. In this case, the daughter's action fulfills a desire her mother had in life but which no longer exists. Isn’t it beneficial to someone to have their desires satisfied, regardless of whether they are alive or dead?Alice: I don’t think so. Whether the desire existed in the past or it's a desire for the future, any desire that no longer exists cannot be beneficial to that person, whenever it might be fulfilled. The daughter's action should not be considered beneficial or harmful to her deceased mother.Bob: Then what about this case? Parents, thinking about their 20-year-old son’s future, advise him to get a professional license. The son, however, currently has no desire for money and prefers volunteer work. The parents predict that in a few years, he will surely change his mind and want money, and they say that he will regret not having gotten the license when that time comes. After much consideration, the son, though he still does not desire money, agrees with his parents' prediction and judges that what he should do now is to obtain the license. <question> Of the following analyses of this conversation, which ones are correct as written in <statements>? <statements> (a) If agreeing with the parents’ prediction and deciding that what he should do now is to obtain the license is acknowledged as reasonable, Alice’s position is weakened. (b) Alice and Bob hold the same view regarding whether damaging a corpse is an act that harms the deceased party. (c) Bob argues that what benefits or harms a person is related to satisfying that person's desires, but Alice does not agree with this claim. <choices> ① (a) ② (b) ③ (a), (c) ④ (b), (c) ⑤ (a), (b), (c)

## Original Answer

**Answer choice:** 1

Only (a) is a correct analysis among <statements>, so the answer is ①.

## LLM Response

Answer-⑤ (a), (b), (c)

Rationale 1. (a) is correct because agreeing with the parents' prediction and deciding to obtain the license fulfills the son's parents' desires, which existed in the past, thereby fulfilling a desire that no longer exists and thus should not be considered beneficial or harmful to them.

Rationale 2. (b) is correct because Alice states that damaging a corpse is an act that harms the deceased party, which is the same view Bob holds.

Rationale 3. (c) is correct because Bob argues that what benefits or harms a person is related to satisfying that person's desires, while Alice disagrees with this claim.
