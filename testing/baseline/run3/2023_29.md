# Question 2023_29

## Original Question

When a principal delegates tasks to an agent, but cannot perfectly observe the agent’s actions, the agent may behave in ways that maximize their own interests at the expense of the principal’s interests. This is called the principal-agent problem. There is an argument that “the principal-agent problem also arises for homeowners who sell their house through real estate agents.” In the United States, real estate agents usually receive a 6% commission on the sale price, but after other participants take their share, the agent effectively receives only about 1.5%. Since the commission is linked to the sale price, it may seem the agent is incentivized to sell the house at the highest possible price. However, this is only true to a limited extent. For example, if the sale price is increased by $10,000, the agent only earns an additional $150. In order to obtain this higher price, the property would need to stay on the market longer, incurring costs for advertising and showing the home to potential buyers. Therefore, rather than paying these extra costs for an additional $150, the agent is incentivized to sell the house at a reasonable price relatively quickly. Homeowners, who have far less information than agents regarding their home’s value or the likelihood of a sale, are prone to accept the agent’s suggestion to settle for a lower price. <question> Which of the following assessments in <statements> is/are correct based on the above passage? <statements> (a). The finding that agents leave their own properties on the market longer than those belonging to others reinforces the argument that the principal-agent problem applies to homeowners selling through real estate agents. (b). The finding that increasing the effective commission rate from 1.5% to 3.5% led to homes spending less time on the market reinforces the argument that the principal-agent problem applies to homeowners selling through real estate agents. (c). The finding that the development of information and communication technology, making housing price information easier and more accurate to obtain, has shortened the time homes spend on the market, reinforces the argument that the principal-agent problem applies to homeowners selling through real estate agents. <choices> ① (a) ② (b) ③ (a), (c) ④ (b), (c) ⑤ (a), (b), (c)

## Original Answer

**Answer choice:** 1

Only (a) is a correct assessment. The answer is ①.

## LLM Response

Answer-③.

Rationale 1. While the commission rate is 6%, the actual amount the agent receives is lower due to other participants taking their share, so (b) is incorrect.

Rationale 2. The passage suggests that the reason agents are incentivized to sell the house at a reasonable price relatively quickly is that they have to incur costs for advertising and showing the home to potential buyers. Increasing the effective commission rate would not address these costs, so (b) is incorrect.

Rationale 3. The passage suggests that homeowners are prone to accept the agent's suggestion to settle for a lower price due to their lack of information. The development of information and communication technology has made housing price information easier and more accurate to obtain, which has shortened the time homes spend on the market, so (c) is correct.
