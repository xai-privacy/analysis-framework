# Question 2024_28

## Original Question

<Theory> People tend to find it easier to process information when it is presented in a familiar order. The feeling that information processing is easy leads to a sense of familiarity with the subject, and as a result, the preference for the subject increases. Since the main ingredient and final product inherently contain the concept of temporal flow in the information itself, if consumers are first presented with the main ingredient of a product and then the final product made from that ingredient, it is expected that consumers’ preference for the product will be higher than if the information is presented in the reverse order.However, this effect does not appear with the same strength for everyone. The lower the familiarity with the product, the greater the effect, and the higher the familiarity, the smaller the effect.<Experiment> A total of 60 men and 60 women, selected randomly, were first surveyed for their familiarity with olive soap. According to the survey results, men generally had low familiarity, while women had high familiarity. The male and female participants were divided into two groups each. One group in each gender was shown an advertisement in which the main ingredient, olive oil, was presented before olive soap; the other group was shown an advertisement in which olive soap was presented before olive oil. Afterwards, the ease of information processing regarding olive soap and the preference for the product were measured for each of the four groups. <question> Which of the following, according to <statements>, is a correct assessment regarding the above text? <statements> (a) If men had a significantly higher preference for olive soap when the information was presented in the ‘olive oil–olive soap’ order, compared to the reverse order, then <Theory> would be supported. (b) If women felt that information processing was easier when the information was presented in the ‘olive soap–olive oil’ order than in the reverse order, but men did not feel this way, then <Theory> would be supported. (c) If, among individuals with similar familiarity with olive soap in all groups, there was no significant difference in product preference between men and women, then this result is compatible with <Theory>. <choices> ① (a) ② (b) ③ (a), (c) ④ (b), (c) ⑤ (a), (b), (c)

## Original Answer

**Answer choice:** 3

Therefore, only (a) and (c) are correct assessments, so the answer is ③.

## LLM Response

Answer-②

1. The statement in (a) assumes that if men had a significantly higher preference, then the Theory would be supported, but it overlooks the fact that the effect may not be as strong in men as in women.
2. The statement in (c) suggests that if there is no difference in product preference between men and women among those with similar familiarity, it is compatible with the Theory. However, the Theory states that the effect is more pronounced in those with lower familiarity, so this scenario does not necessarily support the Theory.
