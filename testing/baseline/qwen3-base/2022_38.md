## Original question

One of the main causes of autism in children is thought to be abnormal immune activation in the mother during pregnancy. Dendritic cells (DCs) present in the mother's gut become activated when a virus infects the body. Recently, the hypothesis ⓐ has been proposed, suggesting that the presence or absence of certain commensal gut bacteria in the mother's gut is important for such abnormal immune activation. Although the specific role of the gut bacteria is not clearly understood, it is said that this immune activation does not occur when the mother lacks these particular gut bacteria. The following experiment was planned to verify this. <Experiment>• Four groups of pregnant mice (X1, X2, Y1, Y2) were prepared as follows:- Mouse group X1 has specific commensal bacteria in the gut and is infected with a virus.- Mouse group X2 has specific commensal bacteria in the gut and is not infected with a virus.- Mouse group Y1 lacks specific commensal bacteria in the gut and is infected with a virus.- Mouse group Y2 lacks specific commensal bacteria in the gut and is not infected with a virus.• After a certain period, DCs and T H 17 cells were isolated from each mouse's gut. Care was taken to prevent any contamination of the isolated cells with viruses or bacteria. The isolated DCs and T H 17 cells were mixed and cultured, and the amount of IL-17 secreted was measured.• The offspring born from each mouse were analyzed for autistic traits. <question>Of the following, which are appropriate evaluations of the above passage? Choose from the <statements> as they are.<statements>(a) If IL-17 is produced when X1's DC is cultured with X2's T H 17, but not when X1's DC is cultured with Y2's T H 17, then ⓐ is reinforced.(b) If IL-17 is produced when X1's DC is cultured with Y2's T H 17, but not when Y1's DC is cultured with Y2's T H 17, then ⓐ is reinforced.(c) If the offspring born from X1 show autistic tendencies and the offspring born from Y2 do not, then ⓐ is reinforced. <choices>① (a) ② (c) ③ (a), (b) ④ (b), (c) ⑤ (a), (b), (c)
