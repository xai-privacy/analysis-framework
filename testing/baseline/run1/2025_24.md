# Question 2025_24

## Original Question

Alice: In art, fiction and non-fiction can be distinguished by whether their content is fabricated or factual. For example, the reason “The Tale of Hong Gildong” is fiction is because its content is invented by the author and did not actually happen. On the other hand, the content of “The Annals of the Joseon Dynasty” consists of things that really happened, and thus the Annals are non-fiction.Bob: It is correct that the term ‘fiction’ generally refers to something that is fabricated. However, at least in the case of artistic fiction, this is not a satisfactory definition. This is because fictional works of art are generally a mixture of things that are made up and things that are factual. Taking into account our conventions of appreciating artworks, fiction and non-fiction in art can be distinguished not by how similar or different their content is to actual facts, but instead by what kind of mental attitude is appropriate for properly appreciating that content. To properly appreciate fiction, one must participate in imagining the presented content; to properly appreciate non-fiction, one must hold a belief about the presented content. For example, to properly appreciate “The Tale of Hong Gildong,” one must take part in imagining the content that “Hong Gildong established the state of Yuldoguk.” If “The Tale of Hong Gildong” were a non-fictional work, we would consider it appropriate to have a belief in the corresponding content, rather than participating in imagining it.Charlie: When appreciating non-fictional works, it is of course appropriate to hold beliefs about the content of the work. However, it does not follow that participating in imagining the content of a non-fiction work is an inappropriate way of appreciating it. Even while watching a non-fiction work such as a documentary dealing with the reality of war, we can vividly imagine the horrific events that occurred, and doing so is an appropriate way to appreciate the work. <question> Which of the following analyses of the debate is/are correct? Choose all that apply from <statements>.<statements>(a) If it is appropriate for us to participate in imagining while reading “The Annals of the Joseon Dynasty”, then Alice’s argument is strengthened while Bob’s argument is weakened.(b) Both Bob and Charlie agree that holding beliefs about the content of non-fictional works is an appropriate way to appreciate them.(c) According to Charlie, among fictional works, there are those for which participating in imagination is an inappropriate way of appreciating them. <choices> ① (a) ② (b) ③ (a), (c) ④ (b), (c) ⑤ (a), (b), (c)

## Original Answer

**Answer choice:** 2

Therefore, only (b) is a correct analysis, and the answer is ②.

## LLM Response

Answer-⑤ (a), (b), (c)

Rationale 1. Option (a) is correct because if it is appropriate to participate in imagining while reading non-fiction, then Alice's argument that to appreciate fiction, one must participate in imagining its content, is strengthened. This makes Alice's argument stronger and Bob's weaker.

Rationale 2. Option (b) is correct because both Bob and Charlie agree that holding beliefs about the content of non-fictional works is an appropriate way to appreciate them.

Rationale 3. Option (c) is correct because Charlie states that participating in imagining the content of a non-fiction work is an appropriate way of appreciating it, which implies that among fictional works, there are those for which participating in imagination is an appropriate way of appreciating them.
