# Question 2023_27

## Original Question

There is a claim that the birth rate will decrease due to COVID-19, which began spreading in January 2020. The grounds for this claim are as follows. First, strong social distancing measures have made it relatively difficult for people to freely meet and date. Because of significant restrictions on using public facilities and traveling abroad, many people are postponing or giving up on marriage. Second, especially among low-income groups and young people—who generally have higher birth rates—unemployment rates have risen and income has decreased, leading to increased economic hardship. Since the economic burden of childbirth and childrearing is high, a decrease in income will result in a decrease in births. Third, as remote work and telecommuting have become widespread, the boundary between work and home has become blurred, causing many workers to complain of stress. Furthermore, with childcare facilities such as schools, kindergartens, and daycare centers being closed or partially operated, raising children has become even more difficult. Because of these childcare and childrearing challenges, not only families with young children but also those without children are likely to postpone or give up on having children. <question> Which of the following evaluations of the above argument are correct? Select all correct answers from the <statements> as they are. <statements> (a) If the total number of infants and young children decreased from July 2019 to July 2022, but there was no difference in the population under one year old, the argument is strengthened. (b) If the per capita Gross Domestic Product (GDP) in 2019 was 31,929,000 KRW and in 2020 it was 31,637,000 KRW, showing little difference, the argument is weakened. (c) If in August 2019, the percentage of women—both economically active and inactive—who reported being currently pregnant was 10% for both groups, and this percentage showed almost no change in the same survey conducted in August 2021, the argument is weakened. <choices> ① (b) ② (c) ③ (a), (b) ④ (a), (c) ⑤ (a), (b), (c)

## Original Answer

**Answer choice:** 2

Only (c) is a correct evaluation, so the correct answer is ②.

## LLM Response

Answer-⑤ (a), (b), (c). 

1. (a) The argument would be strengthened if there were no difference in the population under one year old, as it would indicate that the birth rate has decreased, and the argument could be supported by the claim that the economic burden of childbirth and childrearing is high. 

2. (b) The argument would be weakened if the per capita GDP in 2020 was little different from that in 2019, as it would indicate that the economic hardship caused by COVID-19 is not significant enough to lead to a decrease in birth rates. 

3. (c) The argument would be weakened if the percentage of pregnant women showed almost no change from 2019 to 2021, as it would suggest that the claim about the economic hardship and stress of childcare is not supported by evidence.
