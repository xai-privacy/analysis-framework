# Question 2024_27

## Original Question

Even though jurors infer, based on probabilistic evidence, that it is highly likely the defendant is responsible for the case, there is a tendency for them to hesitate in returning a guilty verdict or a verdict in favor of the plaintiff. There is a <hypothesis> that explains this phenomenon. According to this <hypothesis>, even if evidence that does not explicitly present who is responsible for the event is itself considered valid, its value as information is evaluated as low. Therefore, such information has less influence on the verdicts of jurors. In other words, there is a psychological difference for jurors between testimony such as “There is an 80% probability that the defendant is responsible,” and “Based on evidence that has an 80% probability of being correct, the defendant is responsible.” In order to test this hypothesis, the researchers conducted an <experiment>. In the <experiment>, mock jurors were made to read the following case summary: “Alice sued bus company B for suspicion that B’s bus hit her dog while they were out walking. In the city where Alice lives, there are only two companies: Company B operates only blue buses and Company G operates only gray buses, but Alice is color-blind and could not confirm the color of the bus that caused the accident.” After randomly splitting the mock jurors into two groups, Group 1 was presented only with Investigator's Testimony X, while Group 2 was given both Testimonies X and Y. X: As a result of applying tire matching technology, 8 out of B’s 10 buses and 2 out of G’s 10 buses were a perfect match with the tire tracks collected from the scene of the accident. Y: Based on the tire track evidence, I believe that it was B’s bus that hit the plaintiff’s dog. The mock jurors were then asked to indicate the probability that B’s bus actually hit the dog and to render a verdict on B. As a result of the experiment, the value for ‘subjective probability’—the probability the mock jurors believed B was responsible—was the same for both groups, and the ‘plaintiff verdict rate’—the proportion of mock jurors in each group who concluded that B was responsible—was lower than the subjective probability in both groups. <question> Select, from the <statements> below, only those evaluation(s) of the above text that are appropriate, as they are stated. <statements> (a) If the plaintiff verdict rate in Group 1 is significantly lower than that in Group 2, the <hypothesis> is weakened. (b) If the difference between subjective probability and plaintiff verdict rate is significantly greater in Group 1 than in Group 2, the <hypothesis> is strengthened. (c) If, when testimony from a witness who saw a gray bus hitting Alice’s dog is additionally presented to both groups, the plaintiff verdict rate in Group 2 decreases significantly more than in Group 1, the <hypothesis> is weakened. <choices> ① (a) ② (b) ③ (a), (c) ④ (b), (c) ⑤ (a), (b), (c)

## Original Answer

**Answer choice:** 2

Only (b) is correct, so the answer is ②.

## LLM Response

Answer-②. 

Rationale 1. The hypothesis posits that even if the evidence is valid, its value as information is evaluated as low. This implies that the value of evidence is not necessarily reflected in the verdict rate. If the difference between subjective probability and plaintiff verdict rate is significantly greater in Group 1 than in Group 2, it could indicate that the value of the evidence is not being fully utilized, which would weaken the hypothesis.

Rationale 2. The experiment shows that the value of the evidence was not being fully utilized in both groups, as the plaintiff verdict rate was lower than the subjective probability. This indicates that the hypothesis that the value of evidence is low is supported by the experiment.
