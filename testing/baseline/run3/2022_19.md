# Question 2022_19

## Original Question

The research team surveyed a group of philosophers and a group of laypeople regarding their agreement with the following three statements.(Statement A) If a claim is true for someone, it is true for everyone.(Statement B) If everyone agrees with a claim, then the claim is true.(Statement C) If a claim is true, it represents a fact.In both groups, the proportion of respondents who agreed with (C) exceeded 80%. For (B), the proportion of those who disagreed was much larger in both groups, but the agreement rate was higher among philosophers. What is interesting is (A): 83% of philosophers agreed with (A), whereas only slightly over 40% of laypeople did, and in fact, a higher proportion of laypeople responded that they disagreed. Where does this difference regarding (A) originate? According to the research team, (A) can be interpreted in one of two ways:[Interpretation 1] If it has been determined that a claim is true, its truth is objective.[Interpretation 2] If someone thinks a claim is true, everyone will agree with them.Saying that the truth of a claim is objective means that its truth is not relative to each individual’s perspective. The research team states, "ⓐ The reason the proportion of agreement with (A) among laypeople was significantly lower than among philosophers is that philosophers tend to read (A) as [Interpretation 1], while laypeople tend to read it as [Interpretation 2]." Despite this difference, the research team also believes that "ⓑ Regarding the objectivity of truth, the opinions of laypeople and philosophers are in accord." This is because (A) and (C) are statements that affirm the objectivity of truth, while (B) denies it, and the proportion of agreement with (C) was similarly high among both laypeople and philosophers, while the proportion who disagreed with (B) was also similarly high in both groups. <question> Which of the following evaluations, as given in <statements>, is/are correct regarding the above passage? <statements> (a) If additional research revealed that the vast majority of philosophers responded "disagree" to [Interpretation 2], ⓐ would be strengthened.(b) If additional research revealed that the vast majority of laypeople responded "agree" to [Interpretation 1], ⓑ would be strengthened.(c) If it were found that the difference in agreement rates with (B) between laypeople and philosophers was due to some philosophers who actually disagreed with (B) mistakenly responding "agree," then ⓑ would be strengthened. <choices>① (a) ② (b) ③ (a), (c) ④ (b), (c) ⑤ (a), (b), (c)

## Original Answer

**Answer choice:** 5

Therefore, since (a), (b), and (c) are all valid evaluations, the answer is ⑤.

## LLM Response

Answer-③ Rationale 1. This is because the team states that the difference in agreement rates with (A) does not affect the conclusion that the opinions of laypeople and philosophers are in accord. Rationale 2. The team also states that if additional research revealed that the vast majority of philosophers responded "disagree" to [Interpretation 2], it would weaken the evidence for ⓑ, since this would indicate that philosophers' responses are not in accord with the team's interpretation of their responses. Similarly, the team would be weakened if additional research showed that a large proportion of philosophers mistakenly responded "agree" to [Interpretation 2] or if a large proportion of laypeople responded "agree" to [Interpretation 1].
