# Question 2021_36

## Original Question

In the Seychelles of the Western Indian Ocean lives an endemic species known as the ‘tiger chameleon.’ However, the Seychelles is a remote location, approximately 1,100 km northeast of Madagascar—a large island off the southeastern coast of Africa—and about 2,800 km west of India. How did such a small animal, unable to fly nor particularly skilled at swimming, come to inhabit this place? Two explanations have been presented regarding this question. One is that ⓐ ‘the ancestors of the tiger chameleon originally lived in their current location, but became isolated on islands as the continents separated and drifted.’ The Seychelles was originally part of the supercontinent Gondwana together with Africa, India, and Madagascar; as India-Madagascar and Africa separated, followed by the split between India and Madagascar, and finally the split between India and the Seychelles, the islands reached their current position. According to this explanation, the ancestor of the tiger chameleon evolved independently after the Seychelles separated from other regions. The other explanation, ⓑ, is that ‘the ancestor of the tiger chameleon reached the Seychelles by rafting on floating branches or similar debris from rivers or coasts on Madagascar or Africa.’ According to this, the ancestor originally lived in Africa or Madagascar, and after events such as flooding near its habitat washed it out to sea, it migrated to the Seychelles on floating debris, and then evolved independently. <question> Which of the following <statements> correctly assess ⓐ and ⓑ? <statements> (a) The fact that there are chameleon species that inhabit only the Comoros—a remote island formed by undersea volcanic activity and never connected to a continent—supports ⓐ.(b) If there is research showing that since the Seychelles split from India, the ocean currents in the Western Indian Ocean have flowed from west to east, this would weaken ⓑ.(c) If there is research showing that the most recent common ancestor of East African chameleons and tiger chameleons appeared later than the most recent common ancestor of Madagascan chameleons and tiger chameleons, this would weaken ⓐ but not weaken ⓑ. <choices>① (a) ② (c) ③ (a), (b) ④ (b), (c) ⑤ (a), (b), (c)

## Original Answer

**Answer choice:** 2

Of the <choices>, only (c) is a proper assessment, so the correct answer is ②.

## LLM Response

Answer-④

Rationale 1. Statement (a) does not provide any relevant information to support either explanation, as the isolation of the Seychelles is already accounted for by explanation ⓐ.

Rationale 2. Statement (b) is correct as the direction of ocean currents would make it unlikely for a floating debris to migrate from west to east, and thus supports explanation ⓐ.

Rationale 3. Statement (c) is correct as the difference in the timing of the most recent common ancestors would imply that the ancestors of the tiger chameleon evolved in different places.
