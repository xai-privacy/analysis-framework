# Question 2021_20

## Original Question

Alice: It is not clear whether changes in scientific theories constitute "genuine progress." Of course, if we look at the history of science, there have been instances where a successor theory replaced a previous theory by explaining and predicting a greater number of facts. However, this does not serve as grounds for claiming that the successor theory is “truly progressive.” Those cases merely show that the successor theory received more social support or that more scientists participated in its research.Bob: Even if such external factors influenced past achievements of theories, it is not impossible to judge whether real progress has taken place. This is because the judgment of progress depends not only on past achievements but also on future achievements that could be attained. And the potential achievements that a theory may have in the future are not influenced by such external factors.Alice: It is possible to compare the past performances of theories. However, it is difficult to compare the scope of explanations and predictions they will achieve in the future, that is, the future potential of the theories. This is because we cannot know all the consequences that a theory may have in the future.Bob: We can often compare the future potential of two theories. For example, for two theories T1 and T2, suppose that we can derive all statements constituting T1 deductively from those of T2, but not vice versa. In that case, T2 will make new predictions in addition to all the predictions of T1. In this case, T2 is “more general” than T1, and therefore has more future potential. <question> Which of the following analyses of the debate above is/are correct, as given in <statements>? <statements> (a) Both Alice and Bob agree that, for changes in scientific theory to constitute "genuine progress," the success of a theory should not be explained solely by social factors.(b) Both Alice and Bob agree that changes in scientific theory have occurred in such a way that the successor theory preserves the explanations and predictions of the previous theory and further provides new explanations and predictions.(c) If the theory of relativity made correct predictions in areas where Newtonian theory made incorrect ones, Bob would agree that the theory of relativity is “more general” than the Newtonian theory. <choices> ① (a) ② (b) ③ (a), (c) ④ (b), (c) ⑤ (a), (b), (c)

## Original Answer

**Answer choice:** 1

Therefore, only (a) is the correct analysis, so the answer is ①.

## LLM Response

Answer-⑤ (a), (b), (c)

This aligns with Bob's statement, indicating that they both agree on the importance of considering future achievements in evaluating progress.

Rationale 2. Bob provides an example where comparing the future potential of two theories can help determine which one is more general and therefore has more future potential. This aligns with Alice's statement about comparing past performances of theories, as it shows that they both recognize the importance of considering future potential in evaluating progress.

Rationale 3. Bob specifically mentions that if the theory of relativity makes correct predictions where Newtonian theory fails, he would agree that the theory of relativity is "more general" than Newtonian theory.
