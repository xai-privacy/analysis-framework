# Question 2022_28

## Original Question

The Victim Impact Statement (VIS) system provides victims with an opportunity to express the impact they have suffered from a crime during the sentencing phase of a trial. There is a tendency for sentences to be heavier when a VIS is submitted compared to when it is not, and two views have been suggested regarding the reasons for this phenomenon. According to View A, it is not the presence of the VIS itself but the severity of the harm described in the VIS that leads to heavier sentences. According to this view, the more severe the harm, the heavier the sentence, as victims who have suffered more serious harm are more likely to submit a VIS in order to highlight the details of the harm described in the indictment, whereas victims who have not suffered serious harm tend not to submit a VIS. Therefore, it appears as though there is a meaningful relationship between VIS and sentencing. According to View B, not only are judges or jurors influenced by the harm described in the VIS, but they are also influenced by the strong emotions expressed by the victim through the VIS; as a result, the VIS itself induces a heavier sentence. Two research methods, P and Q, have been devised to test the validity of each view.P: Mock jurors, randomly selected, are divided into three groups and provided with objective information about a case.[Group 1] receives a VIS describing emotional injury that is much more serious than generally expected; [Group 2] receives a VIS describing emotional injury that is less serious than generally expected; [Group 3] receives no VIS. The average sentence given by each group is then compared.Q: Mock jurors, randomly selected, are divided into three groups and provided with objective information about a case.[Group 1] is presented with a video of a trial in which the victim reads a serious VIS in a highly emotional manner; [Group 2] is presented with a video of a trial in which the victim reads the same VIS in a calm manner; [Group 3] is presented with a video in which the victim calmly reads a VIS that is less serious than the previous one. The average sentence given by each group is then compared. <question> Which of the statements in <statements> below correctly evaluate the above text? <statements> (a) If, in P, the average sentence of [Group 1] is significantly higher than that of [Group 2], and the average sentence of [Group 2] is significantly higher than that of [Group 3], View A is strengthened.(b) If, in Q, the average sentence of [Group 1] is significantly higher than that of [Group 2], and the average sentence of [Group 2] is significantly higher than that of [Group 3], View B is strengthened.(c) If, in Q, only [Group 1] and [Group 2] are compared as a modified research method, and there is no significant difference in the average sentence between the two groups, View A is weakened. <choices>① (a) ② (b) ③ (a), (c) ④ (b), (c) ⑤ (a), (b), (c)

## Original Answer

**Answer choice:** 2

Since only (b) is a correct evaluation, the answer is ②.

## LLM Response

Answer-③ (a), (c). 

 Rationale 1. The problem states that in P, the average sentence is higher for Group 1 and lower for Group 2. However, according to View A, the severity of the harm described in the VIS should lead to heavier sentences, not lighter sentences for Group 2. Therefore, statement (a) is incorrect.

 Rationale 2. The problem states that in Q, the average sentence is higher for Group 1 and lower for Group 2. However, according to View B, the emotional expression of the victim should lead to heavier sentences, not lighter sentences for Group 2. Therefore, statement (b) is incorrect.

 Rationale 3. Statement (c) correctly identifies that if only Group 1 and Group 2 are compared, and there is no significant difference in the average sentence between the two groups, View A is weakened, as it suggests that the presence of a VIS should lead to heavier sentences, regardless of the severity of the harm described.
