# Question 2022_12

## Original Question

Country X’s Criminal Act punishes those who steal another person's property as the crime of theft. Regarding the meaning of “property” under the Criminal Act, Alice, Bob, and Charlie are debating as follows.Alice: I think “property” refers to an “object with economic value,” and here, “economic value” means pure economic value, that is, monetary value. Therefore, under the Criminal Act, property is irrelevant to whether the ownership and transfer of the object are lawful.Bob: I agree with Alice that property does not necessarily have to be lawfully owned or lawfully traded. However, since “economic value,” which is a conceptual element of property, is subjectively attached by the owner, I think that as long as the owner’s intention to possess is expressed—regardless of whether there is any monetary exchange value—such an object should be seen as property under the Criminal Act.Charlie: For "economic value,” a conceptual element of property, to be recognized, there must be monetary exchange value. But I believe this is a necessary, not a sufficient, condition. In order for something to be property under the Criminal Act, not only must it have monetary exchange value, but its ownership and transfer must also be lawful. <question> Which of the following analyses regarding the above debate is/are correct? Select all correct options from <statements>.<statements> (a) Alice regards narcotics illegally owned by a drug dealer—which are prohibited by law from being owned—as property under the Criminal Act.(b) Bob regards both the narcotics illegally owned by a drug dealer and a fan letter cherished by a celebrity but not subject to transaction as property under the Criminal Act.(c) Charlie regards a fan letter cherished by a celebrity but not subject to transaction as property under the Criminal Act, but does not regard narcotics illegally owned by a drug dealer as property under the Criminal Act. <choices> ① (a) ② (c) ③ (a), (b) ④ (b), (c) ⑤ (a), (b), (c)

## Original Answer

**Answer choice:** 3

Since only (a) and (b) are correct, the answer is ③.

## LLM Response

Answer-⑤ (a), (b), (c). 

1. Alice is incorrect in saying that property under the Criminal Act is irrelevant to whether the ownership and transfer of the object are lawful, as this interpretation would mean that illegal drugs owned by a drug dealer would not be considered property under the Criminal Act, contradicting the Criminal Act’s definition of theft.

2. Bob is correct in stating that "property" does not necessarily have to be lawfully owned or lawfully traded. He also correctly states that the owner's intention to possess is a conceptual element of property under the Criminal Act.

3. Charlie is correct in stating that the ownership and transfer of property must also be lawful for it to be considered property under the Criminal Act.
