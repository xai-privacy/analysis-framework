# Question 2024_05

## Original Question

A contract is established by the autonomous agreement of the parties. In light of the nature and function of contracts, contract law must not only recognize the autonomy of the parties but also be a normative system that maximally implements and strengthens such autonomy. In what cases can the autonomy of the parties be restricted? There are three views regarding this question. A: Autonomy should be protected because the value lies in autonomy itself rather than the consequences it brings. Since the intention of the parties is inherently worthy of respect, even if the intention displayed by one party differs from what the party originally intended and the other party relies on the displayed intention, the originally intended will should be respected over what was expressed. State intervention is justified only when the exercise of autonomy so fundamentally infringes upon autonomy itself. B: Autonomy should be protected by focusing on the utility it brings to the parties, rather than the value of autonomy itself. A restriction on autonomy is justified if the absolute value of the cost (-) to the parties caused by restricting autonomy is less than the absolute value of the utility (+) gained by the parties. The mere fact that restriction of autonomy increases the utility of a third party (including the State or society) does not justify restricting autonomy. C: Autonomy should be protected by focusing on the utility it brings to society as a whole, rather than its value in itself. This view focuses on the utility to society as a whole rather than to the parties when making evaluations. However, if the absolute value of the cost (-) to the parties caused by restricting autonomy is greater than the absolute value of the utility (+) to the parties, restriction of autonomy is only permissible if the difference is compensated by the State, etc. To the extent of such compensation, the utility of the parties is considered to have increased. <question> Which of the following is NOT a reasonable inference from the above? <choices> ① According to A, even if one party unintentionally expresses intention ⓑ instead of the intended will ⓐ and the other party recognizes ⓑ as the intention, the originally intended will ⓐ should be respected. ② According to B, when restricting the parties’ autonomy is justified, the sum of the cost (-) and utility (+) to the parties is always positive (+). ③ According to C, when restricting the parties’ autonomy, there are cases where the sum of the cost (-) and utility (+) to the parties is negative (-). ④ According to both A and C, even if the sum of the cost (-) and utility (+) to the parties caused by restricting the parties’ autonomy is positive (+), there are cases when the parties’ autonomy cannot be restricted. ⑤ If the Basic Regulatory Act of Country X provides that “the parties’ autonomy may only be restricted if the total utility produced for society as a whole exceeds the costs generated by restricting autonomy,” then this is based on C rather than B.

## Original Answer

**Answer choice:** 3

Only ③ is the incorrect analysis among the <choices>, so the answer is ③.

## LLM Response

Answer-⑤. Rationale 1. C provides that the restriction of autonomy is only permissible if the absolute value of the cost (-) to the parties caused by restricting autonomy is greater than the absolute value of the utility (+) to the parties, and this difference must be compensated by the State. The Basic Regulatory Act of Country X does not explicitly provide this condition, but rather states that the parties' autonomy may only be restricted if the total utility produced for society as a whole exceeds the costs generated by restricting autonomy. This is not necessarily in line with the explanation provided by C.
