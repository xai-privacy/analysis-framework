# Question 2024_22

## Original Question

Alice: Emotions always require an appropriate evaluative belief. Fear of an object is based on the belief that the object is dangerous to me, and sadness over some event is grounded in the belief that the event is a significant loss for me. If I hold beliefs that contradict these evaluative beliefs, then I am either not experiencing an emotion or am both affirming and denying a claim at the same time. Bob: Not only is it fully possible, but it is also in fact common, to experience emotions without holding the appropriate evaluative beliefs. For instance, someone might firmly believe that a spider in front of them is not dangerous to them, yet still feel fear toward the spider. Furthermore, it is evident that animals and infants also experience emotions like fear. However, animals or infants without linguistic ability do not possess evaluative concepts such as “danger” at all, and thus cannot hold beliefs such as “something is dangerous to me.” <question> Which of the following from <statements> can serve as a counterargument to Bob’s line of reasoning? <statements> (a). Not only is it fully possible, but it is also common, to hold contradictory beliefs. There is little difference, either in possibility or in frequency, between holding contradictory beliefs and holding emotions that contradict evaluative beliefs. (b). The claim that emotions always require appropriate evaluative beliefs is not the same as claiming that having such evaluative beliefs is sufficient for feeling the corresponding emotions. Sensations like pleasure or pain are essential aspects of emotion, and animals or infants can easily experience these sensations. (c). Having a concept does not necessarily mean having the ability to express it linguistically. If an animal has a tendency to flee rapidly when a predator approaches, then regardless of whether it knows the word “danger,” it can be regarded as having a belief about the danger posed by the predator. <choices> ① (a) ② (c) ③ (a), (b) ④ (b), (c) ⑤ (a), (b), (c)

## Original Answer

**Answer choice:** 2

Since only (c) is a correct analysis, the answer is ②.

## LLM Response

Answer-{c}. 

Rationale 1. Bob's line of reasoning suggests that having evaluative beliefs is essential for experiencing emotions, but it does not account for the possibility of emotional experiences without evaluative beliefs. Choice (c) provides a counterargument by highlighting the idea that having a concept or belief does not necessarily require linguistic ability, and that an animal's behavior can be seen as an expression of a belief without the presence of evaluative language.
