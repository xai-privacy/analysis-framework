# Question 2023_06

## Original Question

[School Regulations] Article 1 (Disciplinary Actions for Students)1. If a student, while at school, engages in behavior that damages the dignity of a student or tarnishes the reputation of the school, the principal may discipline the student for educational purposes.2. When the principal intends to discipline a student, teachers must be involved in the process, and appropriate procedures such as giving the student or their guardian an opportunity to present their opinion must be ensured.<Situation>While receiving a real-time online class at home, a middle school student, Alice, posted information about a corporal punishment incident under investigation at her school on social media to protect the public’s right to know. This sparked a social controversy. In response, P Middle School intends to discipline Alice in accordance with the [School Regulations]. <question> Which of the following choices correctly state(s) the appropriate judgment regarding the <Situation>? Select all that apply from the <statements>.<statements>(a) The claim that the “school” specified in the [School Regulations] should be regarded solely as a physical space becomes an argument against disciplinary action.(b) The claim that a student's freedom of expression for public interest should be guaranteed without limitation becomes an argument against disciplinary action.(c) The claim that all student activities during class hours should be regarded as activities within the school becomes an argument in favor of disciplinary action. <choices>① (a) ② (b) ③ (a), (c) ④ (b), (c) ⑤ (a), (b), (c)

## Original Answer

**Answer choice:** 5

Since (a), (b), and (c) are all correct judgments, the answer is ⑤.

## LLM Response

Answer-⑤ (a), (b), (c)

Rationale 1. The claim that the “school” specified in the [School Regulations] should be regarded solely as a physical space becomes an argument against disciplinary action. Because the student's actions occurred during the school's online class at home, it can be argued that the school does not have control over Alice's actions outside of its physical space.

Rationale 2. The claim that a student's freedom of expression for public interest should be guaranteed without limitation becomes an argument against disciplinary action. Alice's actions on social media were intended to protect the public's right to know and can be seen as a form of free speech, which is protected by the First Amendment. This freedom of expression should be guaranteed without limitation, and disciplinary action should not be taken.

Rationale 3. The claim that all student activities during class hours should be regarded as activities within the school becomes an argument in favor of disciplinary action. The [School Regulations] clearly state that if a student engages in behavior that damages the dignity of a student or tarnishes the reputation of the school, the principal may discipline the student for educational purposes. In this case, Alice's actions during the online class at home may be seen as damaging the school's reputation and tarnishing its dignity, so disciplinary action is justified.
