# Question 2023_20

## Original Question

Alice: A lie is a statement made with the intention of making the other person believe a falsehood as if it were true. In contrast, a statement that is true but easily misunderstood by the listener is called a “misleading statement.” Should a misleading statement be morally less blameworthy just because it is true, compared to a lie? Bob: Not necessarily. That’s because a lie can be made even without the intention to deceive the other person. For example, suppose everyone knows that A is a murderer, and A too knows that everyone thinks so, but A brazenly insists, “I am not a murderer.” Alice: Let's compare the case where A, who actually killed B, lies by saying “I did not kill B,” and the case where A makes a misleading statement by saying “I risked my life to save B twice.” In both cases, A intends to make the listener believe that A is not a murderer, so in that respect, both involve the intention to make the listener believe a falsehood. That’s why I think misleading statements and lies are equally wrong. Bob: While misleading statements are always wrong in that they are intended to deceive the listener despite being true, lies are not always so. If the mere fact that what someone says turns out to be false is itself morally blameworthy, then scientists would have to be said to be doing something wrong, since some of their predictions later turn out to be false. However, if the scientist sincerely said something at the time, they cannot be morally blamed simply because it turns out to be false later. <question> Among the following analyses, which ones are correct regarding the above conversation? Select all that are correct from <statements>.<statements>(a) Alice agrees that a lie must have the intention to deceive the other person, but Bob disagrees.(b) Alice and Bob both agree that there can be statements that are true but still morally blameworthy.(c) Alice and Bob agree that misleading statements and lies differ in their degree of moral wrongness. <choices>① (a) ② (c) ③ (a), (b) ④ (b), (c) ⑤ (a), (b), (c)

## Original Answer

**Answer choice:** 3

Among the options, only (c) is the wrong analysis; the answer should be ③.
