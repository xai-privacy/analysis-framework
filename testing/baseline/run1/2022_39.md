# Question 2022_39

## Original Question

In the 1970s, American researchers discovered that a surgical procedure, which creates a bypass from the stomach directly to the small intestine, resulted not only in weight loss but also in improved blood sugar control. The research team performed this surgery (bariatric surgery) on 608 severely obese patients and observed that, among those with both severe obesity and diabetes, 83% showed improved blood sugar levels. Furthermore, this improvement in blood sugar occurred within a few days after surgery, rather than as a secondary effect following weight loss.To understand how this mechanism works, it is necessary to understand the function of incretin. Incretin is a substance secreted by the small intestine when food is consumed, which signals the pancreas to secrete insulin. Therefore, when incretin is properly secreted after a meal, blood sugar is well regulated by insulin.Rubino proposed the hypothesis that “ⓐ The improvement in blood sugar regulation after bariatric surgery is due to the existence of a substance in the body that interferes with the effect of incretin.” Ingested food passes sequentially through three parts of the small intestine—the duodenum, jejunum, and ileum—before entering the large intestine. Using experimental mice, Rubino performed bariatric surgery that connected the stomach directly to the jejunum, and observed an improvement in blood sugar without reduced body weight or food intake. Based on this, he argued that bypassing the duodenum may be effective in treating diabetes and inferred the possible existence of “anti-incretin” that interferes with the effect of incretin. <question> Which of the following statements from <statements> is/are correct as an evaluation of the above passage? <statements> (a) If a substance secreted in the duodenum decreases insulin secretion induced by incretin, then ⓐ is strengthened.(b) If a surgery where the stomach is removed and the esophagus is directly connected to the duodenum results in improved blood sugar, then ⓐ is strengthened.(c) If after Rubino's bariatric surgery, a second surgery is performed so that food passing into the jejunum is rerouted back to the duodenum, and the effect of blood sugar improvement disappears, then ⓐ is weakened. <choices> ① (a) ② (c) ③ (a), (b) ④ (b), (c) ⑤ (a), (b), (c)

## Original Answer

**Answer choice:** 1

Since only (a) among the choices is a correct evaluation, the answer is ①.

## LLM Response

Answer-⑤ (a), (b), (c).

 Rationale 1. The passage suggests that bypassing the duodenum leads to improved blood sugar levels, which indirectly supports the idea of "anti-incretin" that interferes with the effect of incretin. Therefore, statement (a) is strengthened. 

2. The passage states that bypassing the duodenum leads to improved blood sugar levels, which is consistent with the surgical procedure described in statement (b). Therefore, statement (b) is strengthened.

3. If a second surgery reroutes food into the jejunum instead of the duodenum and the effect of blood sugar improvement disappears, then statement (c) is strengthened, as it suggests that the duodenum is necessary for the improved blood sugar levels.
