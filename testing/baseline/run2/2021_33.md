# Question 2021_33

## Original Question

Let us accept the following two definitions. (Definition 1) "Event Y causally depends on event X" means that both X and Y actually occurred, and if X had not occurred, Y would not have occurred either. (Definition 2) "Event X is the cause of event Y" means that there exists a chain of causal dependence leading from X to Y. As soon as Alice drank a fatal dose of poison, she fell from the 10th floor of a building, hit the ground, and died. The events A~E are as follows: A: Alice drinks the poison B: The poison spreads throughout her body C: Alice falls from the 10th floor of the building D: Alice hits the ground E: Alice's death From C through D to E, all events actually occurred. However, ⓐ B did not actually occur; that is, Alice died before the poison could spread throughout her body. On the other hand, ⓑ "If C had not occurred, E would not have occurred" is false. If C had not occurred, the chain of causal dependence from A through B to E would have been realized. Thus, it may seem to follow that ⓒ C is not the cause of E. However, even when Z does not causally depend on X, it is possible that Y causally depends on X, and Z causally depends on Y. If C had not occurred, D would not have occurred, and if D had not occurred, E would not have occurred. <question> Which of the following analyses of the passage above is/are correct according to the <statements> given below? <statements> (a) From the passage, "Alice's fall from the 10th floor is the cause of her death" follows. (b) From (Definition 1) and ⓐ, "No event causally depends on B" follows. (c) From (Definition 1), ⓑ, and "If C is the cause of E, then E causally depends on C," ⓒ follows. <choices> ① (a) ② (c) ③ (a), (b) ④ (b), (c) ⑤ (a), (b), (c).

## Original Answer

**Answer choice:** 5

Since all of (a), (b), and (c) in <choices> are correct analyses, the answer is ⑤.

## LLM Response

Answer-{③} (a) "Alice's fall from the 10th floor is the cause of her death" is true, because C (the fall from the 10th floor) and E (death) both occurred, and if C had not occurred, E would not have occurred. Rationale 1. This is a straightforward application of Definition 1. Rationale 2. It is worth noting that this analysis is consistent with the chain of causal dependence described in the passage, where the poison (B) is not directly connected to the fall (C) or death (E).
