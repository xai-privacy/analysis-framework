# Question 2021_17

## Original Question

Alice: If I come to hold a belief based on insufficient evidence, that belief itself may not cause much harm. That belief could ultimately be true, and it might never be externally manifested in actions. However, I cannot avoid committing a crime against humanity by making myself an easy believer. The mere fact that a society holds false beliefs is a serious problem, but a greater problem is that society becomes gullible, loses the habit of examining and carefully investigating evidence, and regresses to a state of barbarism. 'ⓐ To believe something on insufficient evidence is always, everywhere, and for anyone, wrong.' - William Clifford, "The Ethics of Belief" - Bob: "Believe the truth!", "Avoid error!" These are the most important commandments for anyone seeking knowledge. However, these are two distinct rules. And depending on which of these one values more, one’s entire intellectual life may differ. One may prioritize the pursuit of truth and see the avoidance of error as secondary, or conversely, consider the avoidance of error most important and the attainment of truth as secondary. Clifford recommends the latter to us. He tells us that, rather than risking the dreadful possibility of believing a falsehood based on insufficient evidence, we should suspend judgment and believe nothing. As for myself, I don’t think I take Clifford’s side. In any case, what we must not forget is that our attitude toward the obligations related to truth or error is grounded not in evidence but in sentiment. People like Clifford, who say "It is better to never believe than to ever believe something false!" are merely expressing a fear of being naively deceived.- William James, "The Will to Believe" - <question> Which of the following analyses of the passages is/are correct? ## Original Answer

**Answer choice:** 3

Since only (a) and (b) are correct, the answer is ③.

## LLM Response

Answer-③ Rationale 1. Alice's argument hinges on pointing out the potential harm that can arise if society becomes gullible, even if the belief itself may not cause harm. This suggests that her conclusion is supported by the undesirable consequences that will arise if people do not act according to her conclusion. Rationale 2. Bob argues that Clifford's belief in ⓐ is not based on sufficient evidence, as Clifford recommends suspending judgment and believing nothing to avoid the risk of believing a falsehood based on insufficient evidence.
