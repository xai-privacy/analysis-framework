## 2. LEET-Arg Question

```text
The roles of nature and culture in human development are a matter of debate, with opinions divided as follows.<Opinions>Alice: I believe that in order to understand human development, we must start from human nature. This is because there are clear differences between groups, and especially biological characteristics play a decisive role in explaining intergroup differences. In addition, many researchers have proven that differences in intelligence quotient between individuals are due to genetic factors.Bob: The fact that intelligence quotients among groups divided by race, gender, etc. show almost no difference is an undeniable fact accepted in the scientific community. Because the human species is so homogeneous, I think that insisting on intergroup differences based on biological criteria stems from a misguided motivation to uphold unequal social hierarchies. Social hierarchy only limits human variability; humans can be shaped differently depending on culture or social environment. <question> Which of the following in <Statements> properly evaluates the <Opinions>? <statements> (a) Research results indicating that in every society throughout history, crime rates have been overwhelmingly higher in a particular group—males between the ages of 15 and 25—weaken Alice's argument.(b) Research results that various social reforms aimed at fairly developing the abilities of all members of society have failed do not strengthen Bob's argument.(c) Research results showing that, as a result of improving English education programs, the English test scores of most elementary school students increased significantly compared to before the improvement, strengthen Alice's argument and weaken Bob's argument. <choices> ① (a) ② (b) ③ (a), (c) ④ (b), (c) ⑤ (a), (b), (c)
```

## 3. Gold Answer and Original Rationale

```text
Gold answer: 2
```

**preliminaries:** In the <Opinions>, Alice emphasizes the importance of human nature, particularly biological characteristics, in the formation of humans. She also claims that these biological traits cause differences between individuals and groups. On the other hand, Bob emphasizes the importance of culture or social environment in human development. He argues that emphasizing biological traits amounts to supporting social hierarchy and rebutts by saying there are actually very few intergroup differences based on biological factors. He further stresses the potential for human development depending on cultural or social environmental changes.

**statement_1_rationale:** (a) The claim that, throughout history, in every society crime rates have been high in a specific group—males aged 15 to 25—means that in any culture or social environment, there is consistently a high crime rate among a particular age group of males. In other words, regardless of cultural or social environmental change, this research result, which suggests that the biological trait of being a male of a certain age greatly influences crime rates, actually strengthens Alice's argument emphasizing biological characteristics in human development, rather than weakening it. Thus, (a) is not an appropriate evaluation.

**statement_2_rationale:** (b) Various social reforms aimed at equally developing the abilities of all members of society can be understood as attempts to promote human development by changing culture or social environment. Therefore, the finding that such reforms failed does not strengthen Bob’s claim, which argues for the potential for human variability and development through environmental change. Thus, (b) is an appropriate evaluation.

**statement_3_rationale:** (c) The fact that, due to improvements in English education programs, most elementary school students’ test scores increased greatly compared to before the improvements shows that human development can be achieved through changes in environment, especially through changes in systems. Therefore, this research result weakens Alice's argument, which emphasizes biological characteristics in human development, while strengthening Bob’s argument, which emphasizes the importance of environment. Thus, (c) is not an appropriate evaluation.

**answer:** Therefore, only (b) in the <Choices> is an appropriate evaluation, so the correct answer is ②.

```text

* This statement actually supports Bob's argument. If social reforms aimed at promoting equality and fairness have failed, it suggests that cultural and social factors are more influential than

* Therefore, statement (b) is incorrect.

* Therefore, statement (c) is incorrect.

However, if we consider the general trend of the statements, we can see that:

**Statement 4: (b), (c)**

* Statement (b) suggests that social reforms aimed at promoting equality and fairness have failed, which would weaken Bob's argument and strengthen Alice's argument.
* Statement (c) suggests that improving education programs has a positive impact on test scores, which would also weaken Bob's argument and strengthen Alice's argument.
```

## 7. Baseline Result Analysis

- **Correct or incorrect:** Incorrect
- **Main reasoning issue, if incorrect:** The model correctly rejected statement (a) and recognized that statement (c) actually supports Bob rather than Alice, but it mishandled statement (b). It first said failed social reforms would support Bob, then later reversed itself and treated the same statement as part of option `④`. The gold rationale says failed reforms do **not** strengthen Bob because Bob's position depends on the possibility of human development through cultural or environmental change.
- **Notes:** The baseline response is internally inconsistent. It evaluates `(a)`, `(b)`, and `(c)` as incorrect in the statement-by-statement analysis, but then chooses `④ (b), (c)` as the final answer.

## 14. Argumentation Framework Result Analysis

- **Predicted answer:** 2 (`② (b)`)
- **Gold answer:** 2 (`② (b)`)
- **Correct or incorrect:** Correct
- **Did argument extraction help?** Partially. The extracted arguments correctly captured the broad opposition between Alice's biological/nature-based explanation and Bob's culture/environment-based explanation, which was enough to evaluate the answer choices more accurately. However, the extraction simplified Bob's position and did not fully preserve his positive claim that humans can be shaped differently depending on culture or social environment.
- **Did attack-relation extraction help?** Partially. The model correctly identified Bob as attacking Alice, but the graph contained a malformed edge (`Argument 2: Bob → Argument 2: Alice`) and also claimed there was no direct attack between Alice and Bob despite listing Bob-to-Alice attack edges. So the final answer improved, but the attack graph itself was not clean or formally reliable.
- **Main remaining issue:** The model reached the correct final answer, but some reasoning is still unstable. In particular, it says failed social reforms strengthen Bob, then nevertheless marks statement (b) correct. The correct explanation is that failed reforms do **not** strengthen Bob; they weaken or at least fail to support Bob's environmental-development position.

## 15. Baseline vs Argumentation Framework Comparison

- **Baseline outcome:** Incorrect — predicted `④ (b), (c)` instead of the gold answer `② (b)`.
- **Argumentation-framework outcome:** Correct — predicted `② (b)`.
- **Improved / worse / inconclusive:** Improved.

For this question, the argumentation-framework prompting improved the final outcome because it guided the model toward the correct choice `② (b)`, while the baseline chose `④ (b), (c)`. The improvement seems to come from separating Alice's nature-based position from Bob's environment-based position before evaluating the statements. However, the improvement is not fully robust: the attack graph contains a malformed edge and internal contradictions, and the final explanation for statement (b) is not perfectly aligned with the gold rationale. This is a useful example where the structured prompting improves the answer, but the intermediate graph should not be treated as a formally reliable abstract argumentation graph without human review.

## 16. Final Notes

This example suggests that the three-step prompt can help Llama 3.2 3B recover the correct answer even when the baseline fails. The main prompt weakness is in the attack-relation step: the model produced an inconsistent graph, including a malformed Bob-to-Alice edge and a note denying a direct attack that it had just listed. For future prompting, it may help to require a clean numbered argument list and then require attack edges only in a constrained format such as `Argument X -> Argument Y: reason`. It may also help to explicitly ask the model to check whether each statement strengthens, weakens, or is neutral toward Alice or Bob before choosing the final option.
