# Question 2022_26

## Original Question

Suppose a person P has the ability to either perform or not perform the act of eating meat (Act A) and the act of donating to an animal protection organization (Act B), and for convenience, assume there are no other possible acts. If we disregard any mutual influence between performing A and B and only consider the outcome of each act individually, performing A leads to a bad outcome (－80), and performing B leads to a good outcome (+100). If neither A nor B is performed, the outcome is 0 for each. In this case, there will be four 'action combinations' consisting of acts that P may or may not perform. Each combination also has its own outcome value, which is the sum of the outcomes of the acts comprising the combination. For instance, if P performs A but does not perform B, the outcome value of that action combination is the lowest among the four combinations, －80. If certain conditions are met, all acts belonging to that action combination are permitted, so even A, which independently carries a negative outcome value, could be permissible. Regarding the condition under which acts belonging to an action combination are permitted, Alice, Bob, and Charlie respectively assert the following:Alice: A person's act is permissible only if it belongs to the combination among the possible action combinations, as determined by their abilities, that yields the maximum outcome value, and only in that case.Bob: A person's act is permissible only if it belongs to the combination among those the person is actually inclined to carry out in reality that yields the maximum outcome value, and only in that case. However, although P can in fact choose whether or not to perform A and whether or not to perform B, the combination where P does not perform A but performs B is never a combination that P would choose in reality.Charlie: A person's act is permissible only if it belongs to any of the combinations, among those possible based on their abilities, whose outcome value is 0 or positive, and only in that case. <question> Which of the following analyses about the above debate are appropriate? Choose only those that are correct as they appear in <statements>. <statements>(a) According to Alice and Bob, P’s A can never be permitted in any case. (b) According to Charlie, P’s A can be permitted. (c) According to Charlie, there are two action combinations of P that can be permitted. <choices> ① (a) ② (b) ③ (a), (c) ④ (b), (c) ⑤ (a), (b), (c)

## Original Answer

**Answer choice:** 2

Therefore, since only (b) among the statements is appropriate, the answer is ②.

## LLM Response

Answer-④

Rationale 1. (a) and (b) are incorrect because if P can choose whether or not to perform A and whether or not to perform B, then according to Alice and Bob, it should be possible to perform A and B. However, the combination of A and B is not one that P would choose in reality, and therefore A can not be permitted. 

Rationale 2. (b) is incorrect because according to Bob, if P does not choose to perform A, then A can not be permitted.

Rationale 3. (c) is correct. According to Charlie, if the outcome value of a combination is 0 or positive, then the acts belonging to that combination are permissible. Since the outcomes of performing A and B are -80 and +100, respectively, the combination of A and B has an outcome value of +20, which is positive. Therefore, according to Charlie, P’s A can be permitted.
