# Question 2025_30

## Original Question

The various factors influencing a voter’s choice in an election can be broadly divided into ‘policy factors’ and ‘candidate characteristic factors.’ Policy factors are those related to a voter’s policy preferences or ideological tendencies. Progressive voters tend to vote for progressive parties, while conservative voters tend to support conservative parties; this is due to policy factors. Candidate characteristic factors, on the other hand, refer to personal characteristics such as educational background, career experience, and appearance, independent of policy factors. When a candidate from a preferred party is also superior in terms of candidate characteristic factors, the voter’s choice is obvious. However, when the two factors conflict, it is possible for a progressive voter to vote for a conservative party’s candidate, or vice versa. Regarding the relative influence of policy factors and candidate characteristic factors, the following hypothesis is proposed: <hypothesis> In elections where the impact on policy is greater, the relative influence of policy factors is greater than that of candidate characteristic factors. To test this hypothesis, a survey was conducted among individual party supporters, measuring “ⓐ among those who answered that the candidate from a party they do not support is superior in personal characteristics compared to their preferred party’s candidate, the proportion who actually voted for the superior candidate.” In general, the president has a greater impact on policy than individual members of parliament. <question> From the following, which of the statements in <statements> can be inferred correctly? <statements> (a) When the presidential and parliamentary elections were held simultaneously, if ⓐ was higher in the parliamentary election than in the presidential election, this would strengthen the <hypothesis>. (b) If, in the parliamentary election, ⓐ among progressive party supporters was lower when the president was from a conservative party compared to when the president was from a progressive party, then the <hypothesis> would be weakened. (c) If, in the parliamentary election, ⓐ was lower when the majority party in parliament did not change compared to when it did change, this would strengthen the <hypothesis>. <choices> ① (a) ② (b) ③ (a), (c) ④ (b), (c) ⑤ (a), (b), (c)

## Original Answer

**Answer choice:** 1

Only (a) is a correct inference from the options given, so the answer is ①.

## LLM Response

Answer-③.

Rationale 1. The hypothesis states that in elections where the impact on policy is greater, the relative influence of policy factors is greater than that of candidate characteristic factors. Given that the president has a greater impact on policy than individual members of parliament, the presidential election is likely to have a greater impact on policy than the parliamentary election.

Rationale 2. If the presidential election had a higher value of ⓐ than the parliamentary election, it would suggest that the relative influence of candidate characteristic factors is greater in the parliamentary election than in the presidential election, which contradicts the hypothesis. Therefore, option (a) is incorrect.

Rationale 3. If ⓐ among progressive party supporters was lower when the president was from a conservative party compared to when the president was from a progressive party, it suggests that policy factors are playing a stronger role in shaping their voting behavior, which would weaken the hypothesis. Therefore, option (b) is incorrect.

Rationale 4. If the majority party in parliament did not change despite ⓐ among those who answered that the candidate from a party they do not support is superior in personal characteristics compared to their preferred party’s candidate being lower, it would suggest that the influence of candidate characteristic factors is weaker in parliamentary elections, which would strengthen the hypothesis. Therefore, option (c) is correct.
