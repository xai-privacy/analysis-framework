# Question 2023_28

## Original Question

It is known that workers at companies with labor unions earn higher wages than those at companies without unions. This is called the wage premium of labor unions. To explain this phenomenon, there are arguments A and B concerning the wage gaps among workers at non-unionized workplaces (non-union workers), workers at unionized workplaces who are not members (non-members), and workers who are union members (members). A: Labor unions are monopolistic suppliers of labor. Since unions can exercise monopoly power in supplying labor to a firm within the number of their members, they can extract a higher wage than the competitive market where the labor of non-members and non-union workers is traded. Due to this higher wage, labor that could have been employed without union monopoly power is pushed into the competitive market, which again lowers the wage in the competitive market and thus creates a spillover effect that increases the wage premium. B: Labor unions are a system of collective representation for workers. By demanding equal pay for equal work and better workplace safety, unions raise overall worker satisfaction and reduce turnover rates. Furthermore, they prevent arbitrary changes to working conditions and promote work reallocation and capital investment through negotiation. Unions also represent the interests of minorities, thus supporting and considering these groups. Such activities by unions increase workers’ productivity, and this naturally raises overall wage levels at the company. <question> Which of the following statements in <statements> correctly evaluate the above text? Select all that apply. <statements> (a) A survey comparing workers of similar occupations and skill levels found that union members' wages are higher than those of non-members, and there is no wage difference between non-members and non-union workers. This result strengthens A and weakens B. (b) A survey on the gender wage gap among workers of similar occupation and skill level found that the gender wage gap is smaller among union members than among non-members. This result weakens A. (c) A survey of workers at companies with unions found that the wages of union members are similar to those of non-members with similar occupations and skill. This result weakens B. <choices> ① (a) ② (c) ③ (a), (b) ④ (b), (c) ⑤ (a), (b), (c)

## Original Answer

**Answer choice:** 1

Therefore, the answer is ①.

## LLM Response

Answer-⑤ (a), (b), (c)

Rationale 1. If the survey in (a) found that union members' wages are higher than those of non-members, and there is no wage difference between non-members and non-union workers, then this result contradicts statement A, which claims that unions can extract a higher wage due to monopoly power. This strengthens statement B, which claims that unions increase workers' productivity through negotiation and representation. Statement B is also strengthened because the result shows that unions can raise wages among non-members.

Rationale 2. If the survey in (b) found that the gender wage gap is smaller among union members than among non-members, this result supports statement B. Statement B claims that unions support and consider minority groups, which is consistent with the finding that union members have a smaller gender wage gap. This result weakens statement A, which claims that unions exercise monopoly power to extract higher wages.

Rationale 3. If the survey in (c) found that the wages of union members are similar to those of non-members with similar occupations and skill, this result contradicts statement B. Statement B claims that unions increase workers' productivity, which is consistent with the finding that union members have higher wages. However, the result in (c) suggests that this productivity increase is not observed in this specific context, which weakens statement B.
