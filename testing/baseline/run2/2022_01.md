# Question 2022_01

## Original Question

Some people believe that in the not-so-distant future, if neuroscience is able to find the cause of all behavior within the brain, the practice of holding people legally responsible and punishing them will fundamentally change. Their view is that if a person’s criminal behavior is determined by causes in the brain, rather than stemming from free will, then it is not possible to hold that person responsible and punish them. However, this is a misconception arising from a misunderstanding of the law. The law assumes that people generally have the ability to make rational choices. There is general agreement that in order for someone to be exempt from legal responsibility, it must be proven that "the defendant lacks the capacity for rational action." The capacity for rational action here refers to the ability to act in ways that achieve one’s desires based on one’s own beliefs. Neuroscience may be able to help determine whether a person who committed a crime was rational at the time of the crime. However, unless neuroscience shows that people in general do not meet this minimum standard of rationality, it cannot justify a fundamental change in the way the law approaches responsibility. The law is not concerned with whether people possess metaphysical free will or not.
