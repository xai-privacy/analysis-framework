# Question 2022_03

## Original Question

The Constitution is the highest law, establishing the fundamental values of the nation; if a statute violates the Constitution, that law becomes null and void. The question here is which aspect of a statute provides grounds for judging its unconstitutionality. One might say that the literal meaning of the legal text is the core aspect that serves as the basis for unconstitutionality, but in reality, while the Constitution prohibits discrimination, it is rare for a statute to explicitly contain words or sentences that say "discriminate." Fundamentally, the aspect of a law that is judged as unconstitutional is ⓐ ‘the direct consequence that is expected when the legal sentence representing the statute is applied to a specific case.’ For example, if Legal Sentence A contains content punishing those who commit adultery, the deciding aspect for the constitutionality of the law represented by A is the punishment imposed on those who commit adultery as a result of applying A. Some argue that we must examine ⓑ 'the impact that the enforcement of the statute has on the entire society.' For instance, in considering whether a statute giving extra points to a certain group in employment is unconstitutional due to discrimination, it is said that one must also observe the impact such granting of extra points has on others in the society. On the other hand, some still seek the decisive aspect for judgment of unconstitutionality in the meaning of the legal sentence, yet claim that the meaning of the legal sentence is ⓒ 'the contextual meaning that the legal sentence conveys within the context of the society’s history.' Whether a law represented by a legal sentence establishing a women-only educational institution violates the constitutional prohibition on discrimination depends on whether the establishment of such an institution aims to uplift women's status in the given country or is grounded in a historical context of separation and discrimination against women. <Situation> In Country X, after experiencing numerous wars, the national flag has become a symbol of the nation’s very existence, and flag burning is considered a criminal act that violates constitutional order, as it is regarded as an act undermining the authority of the state. However, when Country X’s parliament legislated Law L, represented by Legal Sentence R, to punish those who burn the national flag in order to protect the dignity and authority of the flag, acts of flag burning—which had almost never occurred previously—became frequent as opponents of the law protested, and sympathizers with the act of flag burning also grew in number. <question> From the following <statements>, which ones correctly evaluate the <situation>? <statements> (a) If ⓐ is considered the core aspect providing grounds for judgment of unconstitutionality, then Law L in Country X is unconstitutional.(b) The aspect ⓑ of Law L does not align with the legislative purpose of L as represented by R.(c) If ⓒ is considered the core aspect providing grounds for judgment of unconstitutionality, then Law L in Country X is unconstitutional. <choices> ① (a) ② (b) ③ (a), (c) ④ (b), (c) ⑤ (a), (b), (c)

## Original Answer

**Answer choice:** 2

Thus, only (b) is a correct assessment, and the answer is ②.

## LLM Response

Answer-{④}

Rationale 1. This answer is correct because option (b) aligns with the aspect ⓑ, suggesting that the impact of the statute on the entire society does not align with the legislative purpose of Law L. The frequent occurrence of acts of flag burning and growth in sympathizers indicate that the law's impact on society does not align with its legislative purpose.

Rationale 2. The constitutionality of Law L in Country X is a matter of debate. If we consider option (a), then Law L is unconstitutional because the direct consequence of applying the legal sentence is the increased occurrence of acts of flag burning. On the other hand, if we consider option (c), then Law L is unconstitutional because the contextual meaning of the legal sentence is grounded in a historical context of separation and discrimination against women, which is not aligned with the legislative purpose of protecting the dignity and authority of the flag. Option (d) is incorrect because it suggests that the legislative purpose of L is aligned with the aspect ⓑ, which is not the case.

Note: Options ① and ② are incorrect because they do not accurately reflect the reasoning for the correct answer. Options ③ and ⑤ are incorrect because they suggest that both options (a) and (c) are correct, when in fact, option (c) provides a different interpretation of the situation.
