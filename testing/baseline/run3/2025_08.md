# Question 2025_08

## Original Question

A public good is a commodity that can be collectively used by the public, and its ownership belongs to the people. Anyone can access and use public goods, and no one can be prohibited from using them. However, if public goods are not properly managed, their value inevitably decreases. To prevent such a decline in benefits, the state manages public goods under the name of "administration." However, there is an issue of inefficiency in such cases, so the following <opinion> has been put forward regarding how the state should manage public goods.<opinion>Alice: Since the management of public goods has been completely delegated to the state by the people, the state can manage public goods directly or have a third party do so. In order to efficiently manage public goods and guarantee a quality of service appropriate to the usage price, there is a need to privatize the management of public goods. However, in cases where the state directly manages public goods, citizens have granted the state discretion regarding management methods on the condition that they do not bear any costs exceeding what they would bear under direct state management. The state must satisfy this condition.Bob: Privatization inevitably leads to considerations of profit, so in the end, the cost of using public goods, which was previously managed by the state, will ultimately rise, or there will be a decline in service quality relative to previous prices. Therefore, the state should adopt “co-governance” as an alternative to privatization. Except in cases where it is necessary to prevent a decrease in benefits, the state should not be directly involved in the management of public goods; instead, members of society who use public goods should resolve issues that arise on a self-governing basis. If co-governance in civil society fails, public goods will remain unmanaged. Therefore, members of society must strive to prevent the failure of co-governance. <question> Of the analyses regarding <opinion>, which of the following <statements> are correct? <statements> (a) According to Alice, if, after the privatization of public good X, the usage price exceeds what citizens would have paid if the state had managed it directly, the state cannot make citizens bear the excess portion.(b) According to Bob, if co-governance by members of society fails, the state may become directly involved in the management of public goods.(c) Research indicating that the state can control the usage price of public goods while preventing a qualitative decline in services through privatization would strengthen Alice's opinion and weaken Bob's opinion. <choices> ① (a) ② (b) ③ (a), (c) ④ (b), (c) ⑤ (a), (b), (c)

## Original Answer

**Answer choice:** 5

Since (a), (b), and (c) in the <statements> are all correct analyses, the answer is ⑤.
