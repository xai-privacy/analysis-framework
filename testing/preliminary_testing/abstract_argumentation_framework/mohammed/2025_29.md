## 2. LEET-Arg Question

```text
Various studies on gender inequality in workplaces with a high proportion of men demonstrate that women receive relatively fewer opportunities and lower evaluations compared to men, even when accounting for their actual abilities and achievements. This structural situation faced by women makes it difficult for them to fully exhibit their abilities on teams carrying out projects that require creativity. Creative projects generate synergy when team members gather and engage in active interaction together. However, if women are restricted in their opportunities to demonstrate creativity during team activities, or if their creative expressions themselves are undervalued, women will not be able to fully realize their capabilities within team activities. In this context, “ⓐWomen will display greater creativity in environments where they work separately from men rather than environments where they work together with men.” To test this, the following <Experiment> was conducted.<Experiment>Forty male singers and forty female singers were recruited, and then paired at random to form 40 mixed-gender duets. Of the 40 pairs, 20 were randomly assigned to the control group; each pair recorded the same song in the studio together, accompanied by musicians. The remaining 20 pairs were assigned to the experimental group: the male singer in each pair recorded the same song in the studio with the musicians, while the female singer, alone in the studio, listened to the recording of the male singer and the musicians and recorded her own part, thereby completing the song. There were five musicians in total, all of whom were men, and the same musicians played in all 40 duets. After the recordings were complete, a panel of experts with equal numbers of men and women listened to the songs and evaluated the creative expression of the male and female singers by scoring them. <question> Which of the following in <statements> correctly evaluates the <Experiment>? <statements> (a) If the female singers’ scores were higher in the experimental group than in the control group, ⓐ is supported.(b) If the total score of all female singers was higher than that of all male singers, ⓐ is supported.(c) If the female singers’ scores in the control group were slightly lower than those of the male singers, but in the experimental group the female singers’ scores were higher than those of the male singers, ⓐ is weakened. <choices>① (a) ② (c) ③ (a), (b) ④ (b), (c) ⑤ (a), (b), (c)
```

## 3. Gold Answer and Original Rationale

```text
Gold answer: 1
```

**preliminaries:** The hypothesis presented is that “women will display greater creativity in environments where they work separately from men rather than in environments where they work together with men,” and an <Experiment> is provided to test this. In the <Experiment>, the group where female singers collaborate in singing with male singers and male musicians serves as the control group, while the group where female singers perform separately from the male singer and musicians serves as the experimental group.

**statement_1_rationale:** (a) If ⓐ is true, then female singers will achieve higher creativity scores when not collaborating with men compared to when they do collaborate. Therefore, if the female singers’ scores in the experimental group are higher than in the control group, it means their scores were higher when not working with men, which supports ⓐ. Thus, (a) is a correct evaluation.

**statement_2_rationale:** (b) For example, suppose the results showed that female singers had higher creative expression scores than male singers in the control group, that the scores of female and male singers were similar in the experimental group, and that the male singers’ scores in the control and experimental groups were comparable. Even though the total score for all female singers exceeds that of all male singers, this could actually mean that female singers performed better when collaborating with men, which would oppose ⓐ. Therefore, the statement “the total score of all female singers was higher than that of all male singers” does not necessarily support ⓐ. Thus, (b) is not a correct evaluation.

**statement_3_rationale:** (c) For instance, suppose the male singers’ scores in the control and experimental groups are similar. If female singers scored slightly lower than the male singers in the control group, but outperformed the male singers in the experimental group, this means the female singers’ scores were higher in the experimental group than in the control group, which supports ⓐ, not weakens it. Thus, it cannot be said that “if the female singers’ scores in the control group were slightly lower than those of the male singers, but in the experimental group the female singers’ scores were higher than those of the male singers, ⓐ is weakened.” (c) is not a correct evaluation.

**answer:** Therefore, only (a) provides a correct evaluation, so the answer is ①.

## 14. Argumentation Framework Result Analysis

- **Predicted answer:** 4 (`④ (b), (c)`) as the final option line; however, the explanation says only statement (c) is correct, which would correspond to option 2.
- **Gold answer:** 1 (`① (a)`)
- **Correct or incorrect:** Incorrect
- **Did argument extraction help?** No. Prompt 1 did not actually extract the argument structure of the passage. It repeated the same mistaken answer-choice evaluation from the baseline instead of separating the hypothesis, control condition, experimental condition, and evaluation criteria.
- **Did attack-relation extraction help?** No. The attack-relation response introduced irrelevant entities such as Bob and Charlie from a different question and produced incoherent attack edges. This contaminated the final evaluation rather than improving it.
- **Main remaining issue:** The model failed to understand the causal comparison required by the experiment: female scores in the separate-from-men condition must be compared with female scores in the together-with-men condition. It also confused within-gender comparison with male-vs-female comparison and carried hallucinated argument labels into later steps.

## 15. Baseline vs Argumentation Framework Comparison

- **Baseline outcome:** Incorrect; predicted `② (c)` instead of gold `① (a)`.
- **Argumentation-framework outcome:** Incorrect; final output predicted `④ (b), (c)`, while its explanation suggested only `(c)`.
- **Improved / worse / inconclusive:** Worse

The argumentation-framework approach did not help on this question. The baseline already made the central mistake of thinking higher female scores in the experimental group would contradict ⓐ, and the multi-step approach preserved that same mistake. Worse, the attack-relation step hallucinated irrelevant Bob/Charlie-style argument roles from another type of debate and produced incoherent attack edges. This shows that for experiment-evaluation questions, the model needs explicit prompting to identify the hypothesis, independent variable, control condition, experimental condition, and dependent measure before evaluating the answer choices.

## 16. Final Notes

This case shows a different failure mode from the speaker-based debate questions. Because the passage is an experiment-evaluation problem rather than a direct Alice/Bob/Charlie debate, the generic argument-extraction prompt caused the model to evaluate the answer choices too early instead of extracting the experimental structure. A better prompt for this category should first ask the model to identify the hypothesis ⓐ, control group, experimental group, dependent variable, and relevant comparison. The model also failed to follow the required final-answer format and produced an internally inconsistent final answer mapping.
