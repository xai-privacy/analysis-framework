# Question 2024_18

## Original Question

Alice1: The symbol 'dog' written on paper is about dogs. Likewise, our thought-of-dog in our heads is also about dogs. But just as it's tricky to explain why the arbitrary symbol 'dog' is about dogs, it's also tricky to explain how the thought-of-dog is about dogs. Bob1: That’s simple to explain. If object X causally triggers a certain thought, and if that thought is causally triggered only when X exists, then the thought is about X. Think about something like the elevator indicator. The 7th-floor indicator turns on when, and only when, the elevator reaches the 7th floor. In the same way that the 7th-floor indicator is about the 7th floor, the thought-of-dog is about dogs.Alice2: Your view isn’t satisfactory. For example, suppose Charlie sees a dog. The causal chain from the dog to Charlie’s thought-of-dog is very long. Light reflects off the dog’s fur and enters Charlie’s retina. The retina is activated in a specific pattern, and that information is delivered through the optic nerve to the brain. Finally, the thought-of-dog is formed deep in Charlie’s brain. Let’s call “pattern of activation on Charlie’s retina” ‘d-pattern.' Then we could say that it is the d-pattern—not the dog itself—that causally triggers, and only triggers, Charlie’s thought-of-dog. Bob2: But if you trace back the causal chain leading to that d-pattern, at the very end there is always the dog itself. So Charlie’s thought-of-dog is still about the dog; nothing changes. Alice3: Then let’s say on a foggy evening Charlie mistakes a sheep for a dog and thinks, “There’s a dog beyond that fog.” Now Charlie’s thought-of-dog has been triggered by a sheep, whereas in a normal situation, it would have been triggered by an actual dog, not a sheep. The thought-of-dog can be caused either by the sheep or by the dog. So you’d have to say the thought-of-dog is triggered by a dog-or-sheep. Therefore, if your view is correct, Charlie’s thought-of-dog would be about not the dog, but the object dog-or-sheep. <question> Which of the following analyses of the debate are correct? Choose all that apply from the <statements> below.<statements>(a) Even if the starting point of the causal chain leading up to ⓐ is not the whole dog but just a part of the dog, Alice2’s conclusion would still follow.(b) In Bob2’s argument, he presupposes the principle that, for objects a, b, and c, if a causally triggers b and b causally triggers c, then a causally triggers c.(c) The arguments presented in Alice2 and Alice3 show that, if one accepts Bob1’s view, Charlie’s thought-of-dog could be about something other than a dog. <choices> ① (a) ② (b) ③ (a), (b) ④ (b), (c) ⑤ (a), (b), (c)

## Original Answer

**Answer choice:** 5

Since all of (a), (b), and (c) are correct analyses, the answer is ⑤.

## LLM Response

Answer-③. 1. The conclusion of Alice2's argument that the d-pattern (not the dog itself) triggers Charlie's thought-of-dog is correct because the causal chain leading to the d-pattern indeed ends with the dog itself, making it the object that triggers the thought. 2. Bob2's argument that Charlie's thought-of-dog is still about the dog, regardless of the complexity of the causal chain, is correct as well because it is still the dog that directly causes the thought in the final step of the chain. 3. The argument presented in Alice3, which suggests that if one accepts Bob1's view, Charlie's thought-of-dog could be about something other than a dog, is also correct because if the causal chain leading to the thought can be interrupted or altered by an external object (like a sheep), the thought could indeed be about something other than the original dog.
