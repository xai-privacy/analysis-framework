# Question 2023_03

## Original Question

Alice: The truth that should be pursued in criminal procedure is the substance of the case, that is, the “objective truth.” Moreover, in order to discover the objective truth, the judge’s active involvement and investigative activities are necessary, no less than those of the parties to the case (defendant, prosecutor). Therefore, if it is necessary for discovering the truth and there is no significant procedural violation, the judge can also directly collect or investigate evidence without being restricted by the claims or requests of the parties. Bob: The “substance of the case” or “objective truth” can only be known by God (or an absolute being). Even the conclusion reached by the judge based on the facts and evidence presented by the parties is, strictly speaking, no more than the judge’s subjective truth. The reason why the judge’s subjective truth is trusted and given normative force under the name of a “judgment” is because it is not merely an individual’s subjective truth, but a conclusion derived through a fair criminal procedure. Therefore, what should be pursued in criminal procedure is the “truth through procedure,” and, to that end, the parties must be guaranteed a fair opportunity to contest the truth in court. At this time, the judge’s role should be to interpret, apply, and observe procedure fairly and strictly in order to ensure the truth. In other words, the judge should perform the role of an adjudicator from the neutral position of a third party, making decisions based on the arguments and proof presented by the parties within the prescribed procedure. Charlie: Objective truth exists, and criminal procedure is a process established to approach such objective truth as closely as possible. Accordingly, I basically agree that criminal procedure should pursue objective truth by clearly revealing the facts of the case. However, the discovery of objective truth should be entrusted entirely to the parties’ submission and proof of evidence, and it is undesirable for the judge to intervene directly and actively in this process of discovering the truth. Therefore, as a rule, the judge should perform the role of a neutral adjudicator as a third party; however, since objective truth obtained through human rights violations cannot acquire legitimacy, the judge also has the role and duty to supervise the criminal procedure so that human rights violations do not occur in its course and to rectify them if they do occur. <question> Which of the following analyses concerning the debate above is correct? Choose as listed in <statements>. <statements> (a) Alice and Charlie would support the proposed revision of the law to remove the limit on the number of times the detention period could be extended for the purpose of investigating a crime. (b) Regarding the principle of law that "evidence collected by violating the legitimate procedures prescribed by law, even if it is the only and clear proof of guilt, cannot be used as evidence of guilt without exception," Bob agrees, but Alice would disagree. (c) As for the legal principle that "if the defendant does not appear in court, unless special rules apply, the trial cannot proceed," Bob and Charlie would agree. <choices> ① (a) ② (b) ③ (a), (c) ④ (b), (c) ⑤ (a), (b), (c)

## Original Answer

**Answer choice:** 4

Among the <choices>, only (b) and (c) are correct, so the answer is ④.

## LLM Response

Answer-③ (a), (c). 

Rationale 1. Alice and Charlie would both support the removal of the limit on the number of times the detention period could be extended for the purpose of investigating a crime, as both emphasize the pursuit of objective truth and the need to ensure the integrity of the investigative process. This would align with Charlie's suggestion to trust the parties to submit evidence and proof for the discovery of objective truth, and Alice's belief in the necessity of the judge's active involvement in the investigation process.

Rationale 2. Regarding Bob's agreement on the principle that "evidence collected by violating the legitimate procedures prescribed by law, even if it is the only and clear proof of guilt, cannot be used as evidence of guilt without exception," it is Alice who would likely agree with this principle, as it aligns with her emphasis on the importance of the judge's active involvement in the investigation process and the need to ensure that the evidence is obtained in accordance with the law. 

Rationale 3. Bob and Charlie would agree on the principle that "if the defendant does not appear in court, unless special rules apply, the trial cannot proceed," as both acknowledge the importance of fair procedure and the need for the parties to be guaranteed an opportunity to contest the truth in court. This aligns with Bob's suggestion for the judge to interpret, apply, and observe procedure fairly and strictly, and Charlie's emphasis on the importance of the judge's role as a neutral adjudicator.
