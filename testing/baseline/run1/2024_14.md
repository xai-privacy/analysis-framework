# Question 2024_14

## Original Question

In the process of public opinion formation, it is increasingly common for personal beliefs or emotions to wield greater influence than the truth. A typical example is when posts on social networking platforms—written based on personal feelings yet differing from the facts—still exert significant social influence. These phenomena manifest in various ways, one of which is when people claim something as true without verification, even though it is possible to check the authenticity of the information. We may tend to think simply that, in such cases, the speaker is to be held responsible. However, according to A, "ⓐEven in such cases, responsibility does not always lie with the speaker.” For example, suppose that under normal observation conditions, Alice looks down from the rooftop of a tall building and sees what appears to be a crowd of people gathered in the plaza next to the building. Alice then claims, "There are many people gathered in the plaza." However, in reality, what was in the plaza were not people, but human-shaped mannequins installed for an event. Since Alice's observation felt clear to her, she did not take the simple step of going down to the ground floor to check the facts. Had Alice herself felt suspicious of her evidence or had someone else requested verification, she would have checked, but she was so confident in her experience that she did not feel the need. According to A, even if Alice's claim is not true in this case, it is difficult to assign responsibility to Alice. A accepts the premise that responsibility is assigned to the agent only in cases where the act itself is subject to blame. According to A, in the above example, Alice’s act of claiming "there are many people gathered in the plaza" is, in itself, not blameworthy. Alice's assertion is merely a cognitive error. Therefore, responsibility is not assigned to Alice. A states that in cases of believing or asserting something untrue as the truth, what is crucial is not a permissible level of negligence or cognitive error, as in the above example, but rather an active attitude of intentionally avoiding or refusing to acquire information. Responsibility is, of course, assigned to an agent with such an attitude. It is clear that someone who intentionally refuses even a very simple verification process and spreads fake news warrants responsibility. <question> Which is the correct evaluation of ⓐ? <choices> ① If an act that is not, in itself, subject to blame is not cognitive error, then ⓐ is weakened. ② If it is not always the case that responsibility is assigned to someone who believes in and spreads fake news, then ⓐ is weakened. ③ If there are cases where responsibility is not assigned to the agent of an act that is not, in itself, subject to blame, then ⓐ is weakened. ④ If all false assertions made under normal observation conditions always stem from an active avoidance attitude, then ⓐ is strengthened. ⑤ If in situations where it is impossible to check the truth, acts that are merely cognitive errors cannot occur, then ⓐ is strengthened.

## Original Answer

**Answer choice:** 1

Only ① is the correct analysis among the <choices>, so the answer is ①.

## LLM Response

Answer-③. 

Rationale 1. The statement ⓐ starts with the assertion that responsibility is assigned to an agent only in cases where the act itself is subject to blame. The key point in the text is that A emphasizes the importance of an "active attitude of intentionally avoiding or refusing to acquire information" in determining responsibility. In other words, it's not the act of asserting something untrue that is the issue, but rather the attitude behind it.

Rationale 2. The author provides a hypothetical example to support this point, arguing that in cases where someone claims to see something that is not actually there (in this case, the mannequins in the plaza), the speaker's act of claiming is not blameworthy, but their attitude is. The speaker's confidence in their observation leads them to avoid verifying the information. This suggests that the statement ⓐ is not weakened by the fact that responsibility is not always assigned to someone who believes in and spreads fake news, as this example highlights a different aspect of the attitude that A emphasizes.
