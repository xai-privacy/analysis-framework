# Question 2024_21

## Original Question

Art criticism is a linguistic activity that evaluates works of art. A critic pays attention to the structural features or the representational and expressive properties of a work, and on this basis, presents reasons that provide the grounds for aesthetic evaluation of the work. Based on the following <Critique>, Alice and Bob present their views.<Critique>- Evaluation: Michelangelo’s <Pietà> is excellent.- Reason: Michelangelo’s <Pietà> looks lifelike.Alice: Someone who thinks the evaluation in <Critique> is valid might claim that the standard "If an artwork W looks lifelike, then W is excellent" was applied in <Critique>. However, this standard cannot be applied to Warhol's <Brillo Box>. Even though <Brillo Box> has the same appearance as an actual soap box, that does not necessarily make it excellent. Therefore, it cannot be established that "If property F is attributed to artwork W, then W is excellent" serves as a general criterion for criticism.Bob: There is no generalized standard of criticism that can be applied without exception to all works of art. However, works of art can be categorized as belonging to at least one specific category. In that case, we can find a generalized standard of criticism that guarantees excellence, in the sense that a work realizes the purpose of its category—for example: "For an artwork W belonging to category C, if property F contributes to the purpose of C, then F makes W excellent." The evaluation in <Critique> can be seen as applying the standard: "For artwork W belonging to the category of Renaissance sculpture, as long as the property of 'lifelikeness' contributes to the purpose of Renaissance sculpture, 'lifelikeness' makes W excellent."<question> Which of the following analyses about the above text is correct? Select all correct answers as indicated in <statements>.<statements>(a) According to Alice, a standard for criticism cannot be generalized in any way, so evaluations must always be made only from the perspective of individual works.(b) When evaluating a painting, the judgment "Any work of art with unity is excellent, so this work is excellent" applies the 'generalized standard for criticism' advocated by Bob.(c) The claim, "Even if 'an implausible plot,' which is a typical attribute that undermines the excellence of a play, contributes to the purpose of the Theater of the Absurd, there still cannot be a generalized standard for criticism of the Theater of the Absurd," does not contradict Alice’s position but contradicts Bob’s position. <choices>① (a) ② (c) ③ (a), (b) ④ (b), (c) ⑤ (a), (b), (c)

## Original Answer

**Answer choice:** 2

Since only (c) is a correct analysis, the answer is ②.

## LLM Response

Answer-{④ (b), (c)}

Rationale 1. Alice argues that there is no generalized standard of criticism that can be applied without exception to all works of art. This means that any standard of criticism, if applicable, can only be specific to a particular category of artworks. Hence, Alice cannot support the generalization of standards of criticism.

Rationale 2. Bob suggests that within a specific category of artworks, a generalized standard of criticism can be found. For instance, for Renaissance sculpture, "if the property of 'lifelikeness' contributes to the purpose of Renaissance sculpture," then "lifelikeness" makes the work excellent. This is not a generalized standard applicable to all artworks but rather a specific standard for a particular category.
