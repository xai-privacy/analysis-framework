## Original Question

A species is the basic unit for classifying living organisms. One of the definitions of a species is “ⓐ a group of populations of individuals that are reproductively isolated from other groups, and able to interbreed within the group.” According to Darwin’s theory of evolution, variation accumulates through the process of evolution by natural selection, leading to reproductive isolation and, as a result, speciation, that is, the formation of new species. This definition of species applies relatively well to animals that reproduce sexually, but does not apply to organisms that reproduce asexually or are extinct. In addition, in the case of plants, even if they have an abnormal number of chromosomes, which would generally not allow proper development in animals, normal development may still take place, resulting in a new species different from the parent. Dr. Woo Jang-Choon proposed the theory of species synthesis, suggesting that new species may evolve through hybridizations between different species. Dr. Woo explained the genetic relationships among cabbage, Chinese cabbage, black mustard, rapeseed, leaf mustard, and Ethiopian mustard in the genus Brassica using “ⓑ Woo’s Triangle Model.” According to this model, through natural hybridization between two different species within the genus Brassica, the chromosomes of one species are added to those of another species, resulting in a new species possessing chromosomes from both. For example, when Chinese cabbage (n=10) and cabbage (n=9) are hybridized, rapeseed (n=19) is created. When Chinese cabbage and black mustard (n=8) are hybridized, leaf mustard (n=18) is created, and when black mustard and cabbage are hybridized, Ethiopian mustard is created. <question> Which of the following analyses of the text is/are correct? Choose all that apply from the <statements> below. <statements> (a) The number of chromosomes (n) in Ethiopian mustard is greater than the number of chromosomes (n) in black mustard. (b) According to ⓐ, Chinese cabbage and cabbage cannot be considered different species, and according to ⓑ, black mustard and leaf mustard can be considered different species. (c) When a Maltese (n=39) and a Poodle (n=39), which are classified as the same species by ⓐ, produce a Maltipoo (n=39), the theory of species synthesis used in ⓑ would classify the Poodle and the Maltipoo as different species. <choices> ① (a) ② (c) ③ (a), (b) ④ (b), (c) ⑤ (a), (b), (c)
