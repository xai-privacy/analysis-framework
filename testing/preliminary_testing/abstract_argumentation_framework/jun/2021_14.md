## Question

> \<Theory\> For an act to "cause harm" to someone means that, if the act had not occurred, that person would have been in a better state. Similarly, for an act to "benefit" someone means that, if the act had not occurred, that person would have been in a worse state. Alice and Bob have the following debate regarding \<Theory\>: Alice1: I could have given my friend 50,000 won for no reason but chose not to. If I had done so, my friend would have been in a better state. According to \<Theory\>, my act harmed my friend. But that seems unreasonable. Bob1: \<Theory\> doesn't have such implications. That's because doing nothing—like simply not giving your friend 50,000 won—cannot be considered an act. Alice2: If you limit "act" in such a way within \<Theory\>, other unreasonable consequences follow. Consider someone who saw a child struggling in water and had the ability to rescue them but chose not to, resulting in the child's drowning. Not saving the child clearly harmed the child. Bob2: But this case is different. The choice not to save the child is itself the result of a deliberate decision to avoid the rescue. In this sense, not saving the child should be considered an act. Alice3: Then what about this case? Imagine A bought a gift intended for B but became attached to it and decided to keep it instead of giving it to B. In this case, although not giving the gift was a deliberate decision, it doesn't seem like A harmed B. \<question\> Which of the following statements in \<statements\> is/are correct as an analysis of the above passage? \<statements\> (a) According to Alice1’s interpretation of \<Theory\>, if I could have punched my friend and broken their nose but did not do so, then, by not punching my friend, I performed an act that benefited my friend. (b) Alice2 and Bob2 differ in their judgment about whether not rescuing the child caused harm to the child. (c) If Bob, in response to Alice3, were to assert that “A’s not giving the gift to B did, in fact, harm B,” this would make Bob’s position inconsistent. \<choices\> ① (a) ② (b) ③ (a), (c) ④ (b), (c) ⑤ (a), (b), (c)

## Original Rationale

**Preliminaries**

\<Theory\> is a counterfactual comparative account that explains what it means for an act or event to cause harm or benefit to someone. According to this view, to say that an act causes harm (or benefit) to someone means that if the act had not occurred, the person would have been better (or worse) off. Alice is trying to challenge this view through a series of counterexamples, while Bob defends the view by clarifying the definition of "act." The structure of the debate is as follows: Alice1: Presents the failure to give 50,000 won to a friend as a counterexample to \<Theory\>. Bob1: Responds that this does not count as an act, so it’s not a true counterexample. Alice2: Raises a new counterexample involving not saving a drowning child, arguing that Bob1’s solution is inadequate. Bob2: Adds further conditions to the definition of "act" to show the case of not saving the child is not a counterexample. Alice3: Presents a case with additional conditions to pose another counterexample. \<choices\>

**Statement (a) Rationale**

(a): Alice1 interprets "doing nothing" as an act; thus, according to \<Theory\>, if you do not give your friend 50,000 won, your friend is worse off, making the omission an act that harms your friend. Applying this same logic, if you could have punched your friend and broken their nose but did not, "not punching your friend" is considered an act. Since punching would have made your friend worse off, \<Theory\> would interpret not punching as benefiting your friend. Therefore, (a) is a correct analysis.

**Statement (b) Rationale**

(b): In the scenario with the drowning child, Alice2 claims that not rescuing the child clearly harmed the child and that this case is a counterexample to Bob1’s interpretation of \<Theory\>. Bob2 agrees with the assessment that not rescuing caused harm but claims, through a reinterpretation of \<Theory\>, that this is not a counterexample. Therefore, (b) is not a correct analysis.

**Statement (c) Rationale**

(c): In response to Alice3, Bob could maintain consistency by sticking with the interpretation that "A’s not giving the gift to B counts as harming B." Maintaining this stance would not be inconsistent with Bob2’s previously stated position. Therefore, (c) is not a correct analysis.

**Answer**

Only (a) is a correct analysis among \<choices\>, so the answer is ①.
